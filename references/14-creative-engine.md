# 14 - The Creative Engine (how to have genuinely good ideas)

Strategy tells you what to say. Creativity decides how to say it so that it cuts through, sticks, and moves people. Most AI copy is competent and forgettable because it takes the first idea. A senior creative never does. This file is the process for producing and choosing ideas that are fresh, true and effective.

## 1. What "creative" means in copywriting

Creative copy is **relevant, true, surprising, and simple.** It is not randomness or decoration. A creative idea:
- starts from a **real human truth** about the customer,
- connects it to a **real benefit** of the product,
- expresses it in a way the reader has **not heard before**,
- is **simple enough** to repeat.

Test: "Would the reader repeat this to a friend, and could only this brand say it?"

## 2. The creative process (diverge then converge)

1. **Absorb:** brief, research, Language Bank, competitor matrix, objections. Re-read the customer's words until patterns appear.
2. **Define the problem as a creative question:** "How might we make [reader] feel/see/do [shift]?" Write 3 versions; pick the sharpest.
3. **Diverge:** generate many ideas fast, without judging (section 5). Quantity first.
4. **Incubate:** step away from the first batch (in a session, change task or reframe), then return.
5. **Converge:** cluster, select 3 to 5 with the criteria in section 12.
6. **Develop:** write headline, body, CTA, and a visual or tone direction for each.
7. **Stress-test:** swap test, reverse test, "so what", and "who else could say this".
8. **Polish and ship** with a test plan.

Rule: **do not accept the first idea.** The first idea is usually the most common idea.

## 3. From insight to idea (the tension method)

A strong idea lives in a **tension**: something the customer wants versus something stopping them, or two things in conflict.

Steps:
1. **Customer truth** (an observation about behaviour or feeling): "Students buy materials the night before an exam, then feel guilty."
2. **Product truth** (a fact about the offer): "Instant download by mobile money."
3. **Tension:** "They want to prepare but always start too late."
4. **Reframe:** turn the tension into a promise, enemy, or role: "We are the late-night revision rescue."
5. **Express:** "Exam tomorrow? You still have tonight." / "The 11 p.m. study library."

Template: **People think/feel/do [A]. But actually/also [B]. So our product lets them [C].**

Insight sources: contradictions in the Language Bank, rituals, small embarrassments, hidden costs, status anxieties, social pressures, generational gaps, moments of truth ("the second the file doesn't open").

## 4. Idea-generation techniques (with prompts)

Use at least four per job. Write quick, rough outputs.

| Technique | How | Prompt example |
|---|---|---|
| **Inversion** | Say the opposite or the worst version | "How would we guarantee a terrible haircut?" then flip |
| **Enemy naming** | Give the pain a face | "Name the villain: Load-shedding, the Queue, the Hidden Fee" |
| **Exaggeration** | Push the benefit to an absurd level, then dial back | "So fast the kettle hasn't boiled" |
| **Analogy / metaphor** | Compare to something familiar | "A bank account that behaves like a savings group" |
| **Forced connection** | Combine the product with a random object/place/ritual | "Our delivery + a boda + a wedding" |
| **Role reversal / POV shift** | Write from the product, the competitor, the customer's mother, the clock | "The phone screen speaks" |
| **Specific-to-universal** | Start with an oddly specific scene, then reveal the universal truth | "3:07 a.m., generator off, deadline 8:00" |
| **Constraint play** | Impose limits (6 words, one image, no adjectives) | "Say it in a boda-boda sticker" |
| **SCAMPER** | Substitute, Combine, Adapt, Modify, Put to other use, Eliminate, Reverse | "Eliminate the queue: what's left?" |
| **Before/After/Bridge** | Dramatise the change | "Monday: spreadsheet chaos. Friday: one WhatsApp." |
| **Myth-busting** | Challenge a belief | "'Cheap means bad.' Not when [reason]." |
| **Proof-as-idea** | Make the demonstration the idea | "We repair it in front of you." |
| **Number as idea** | A striking, true number | "Only 3 of 10 books on this shelf are original" (if true) |
| **Ritual creation** | Name a habit the product owns | "Sunday Prep", "The Friday Fresh" |
| **Customer-as-hero** | Make the reader the protagonist | "You didn't come this far to quit at supplementary" |
| **Local truth** | Use a genuine local observation | "Traffic at Nakawa. We deliver before the jam." (verify true) |
| **Question the category** | Ask what the category assumes | "Why do salons make you wait? We don't." |
| **Emotion map** | Name 5 feelings in the journey, write a line for each | "Panic -> relief -> pride" |

## 5. The 100-idea rule and idea ladders

- For headlines or taglines, **write 50 to 100 options** before selecting. Ideas 1 to 20 are obvious; 20 to 60 are where fresh ones appear.
- **Ladder** an idea: write it plain, then 3 more specific, then 3 more emotional, then 3 more surprising. Choose from the ladder.
- **Combine** the best parts of two ideas.
- **Rewrite the winner 10 ways** (tone, length, point of view, device) before locking.

Process for a session: dump -> cluster -> highlight 5 -> rewrite -> test against criteria -> final 3.

## 6. Ad concept formats (the structures ideas live in)

Use these as vehicles; each can carry many ideas.

1. **Problem/solution (demo):** show the pain and the fix in 10 seconds.
2. **Before/after:** visual or narrative transformation (only with honest evidence).
3. **Testimonial / customer story:** a real person, a specific result.
4. **Day-in-the-life:** the product woven into a routine.
5. **Comparison / versus:** product against a habit, not necessarily a brand.
6. **Myth vs fact / mistake list:** "3 mistakes that cost you customers".
7. **Challenge / dare:** "Find a cheaper genuine one. We'll refund the difference." (only with real policy)
8. **POV / skit:** short relatable scene with a twist.
9. **UGC / raw review:** unpolished authenticity.
10. **Explainer / how it works:** 3 steps, simple visuals.
11. **Founder-to-camera:** the owner speaks plainly.
12. **Behind the scenes / process reveal.**
13. **Countdown / event / seasonal hook** (real dates).
14. **Social proof stack:** quick cuts of reviews, photos, numbers.
15. **Question-led hook:** "Why is your WhatsApp full but your till empty?"
16. **Listicle / carousel:** teach, then pitch.
17. **Giveaway / referral mechanic** (comply with platform rules).
18. **Character / mascot** (consistent recurring persona).
19. **Contrast cut:** two realities side by side.
20. **Poll / quiz hook.**

For every concept, define: **hook, tension, payoff, proof, CTA, and the single thing the viewer should remember.**

## 7. Language craft: rhythm, imagery, wordplay, devices

- **Rhythm:** vary sentence length; short sentences hit. Read aloud.
- **Concrete imagery:** a specific image beats an abstract claim ("a freezer full of fresh tilapia" vs "fresh produce").
- **Verbs do the work:** choose precise, active verbs.
- **Devices:** contrast (antithesis: "Less queue. More cut."), repetition (anaphora), parallelism, the rule of three (use sparingly), rhetorical question, understatement, juxtaposition, alliteration (light touch), callback (return to an earlier line), the turn (setup then twist).
- **Wordplay and puns:** good if clear on first read and not at the expense of the benefit; test with someone who did not write it; avoid puns that need explaining or that fail in translation.
- **Specificity:** unexpected specifics (odd numbers, brand names, times) are memorable and credible.
- **Voice:** write as a person speaking to a person.
- **White space and line breaks:** visual rhythm on mobile.
- **Cut the throat-clearing:** begin at the interesting moment.

## 8. Humour, emotion, surprise, and restraint

- **Humour** works when it reveals a truth, fits the brand, and does not mock the customer. Observational humour about shared frustrations works well. Avoid humour on sensitive topics (health crises, finances in hardship, grief, religion, ethnicity).
- **Emotion** drives action: pride, relief, belonging, fear of loss, hope, love, status, curiosity. Name the emotional job, then deliver it with a specific scene, not a claim.
- **Surprise** earns attention: a twist, an unexpected comparison, a reversal. It must resolve into a benefit.
- **Restraint** signals confidence: sometimes the best line is the plain one, with the creative energy spent on the idea, not the ornament.
- **Tone check:** would the customer feel respected?

## 9. Taglines and slogans

A tagline compresses the positioning into a memorable line. Types:
- **Benefit:** "Done before lunch."
- **Promise:** "Open or refunded."
- **Identity:** "For those who build."
- **Challenge:** "Beat the queue."
- **Descriptor:** "Genuine spares. Fair prices."
- **Provocation:** "Cheap is expensive."
- **Invitation:** "Come hungry."
- **Rhythm/rhyme/alliteration:** use only if natural.

Process: write 50, shortlist 5, test: memorable, true, ownable, short (3 to 7 words), says something, works in audio and on a sign, does not need explanation, survives the swap test. Provide 5 finalists with reasoning and a recommended pick.

## 10. Naming (products, offers, businesses)

Process:
1. Brief the name: audience, positioning, tone, geography, languages, channel (domain, handle, radio).
2. Generate 100+: descriptive, evocative, compound, invented, acronym, metaphor, local-language, founder-based, benefit-based.
3. Filter: easy to spell and say, fits mobile typing, no negative meanings in local languages, trademark/domain/handle availability check, distinct from competitors, scalable.
4. Test with 5 target customers: "What do you think this business sells? How do you spell it after hearing it?" (spelling test).
5. Recommend 3 with rationale and tagline pairings.

For **offers**, name the package (The Starter Kit, The Revision Sprint, The Weekend Wedding Package): named offers are easier to ask for and remember.

## 11. Cultural relevance without cliche

- Use **real, current, local references** (places, routines, seasons, language) that the audience actually uses; verify they are current and respectful.
- Avoid **stereotypes**, forced slang, flag-waving, and "exotic" imagery.
- Cultural relevance should serve the **message**, not decorate it.
- When using local language or humour, **have a native speaker review**.
- Respect religion, tribe, politics, grief and class.

## 12. Evaluating ideas: the kill criteria

Score finalists 1 to 5 on: **Relevance** (to the customer's real motivation), **Truth** (to product and proof), **Distinctiveness** (swap test), **Simplicity** (one idea), **Memorability** (repeatable), **Emotional pull**, **Scalability** (can it carry a campaign), **Feasibility** (budget, production, legal), **Measurability**.

Kill an idea if: it relies on an unprovable claim; it is funny but off-benefit; it needs explanation; competitors could run it unchanged; it insults or excludes the audience; it could be misread in a harmful way; it needs a budget the client lacks.

## 13. Making the idea travel (campaign thinking)

A big idea should flex across channels: a **core line**, a **visual or voice device**, **content series**, **hooks**, **offers**, and **proof formats**. Define the "campaign grid": rows = channels (page, ad, WhatsApp, radio, poster, email), columns = stages (attract, consider, convert, retain), cells = the idea's expression. Keep consistent identifiers (phrase, colour, character, jingle, sign-off).

## 14. Creativity under constraints (small budget, low-tech)

- Great small-business ideas are **hyper-specific, human and proof-led**, not expensive.
- Use **the owner's real face and voice,** real customers (with consent), the real shop, and one memorable device.
- Poster, radio spot, WhatsApp status, and boda sticker can all carry the same simple idea.
- **Constraint:** if you only had one sentence on a wall, what would it be?
- **Demonstration beats decoration:** do the thing in front of the camera.
- Use **repeatable formats** (a weekly series) so you create once, reuse often.

## 15. Anti-patterns

- Choosing the first idea; stopping at 5 headlines.
- Cleverness that hides the benefit.
- Borrowing a trending format with no tie to the product.
- Brand-centred slogans ("Excellence in everything").
- Mixing several ideas in one ad.
- Humour that depends on stereotypes or mocking customers.
- Claims no one can believe.
- Copying a competitor's concept with different words.
- Over-explaining a joke or metaphor.
