## 1. Positioning yourself

- **Niche beats generalist.** "Copywriter" is crowded; "WhatsApp sales copy for Kampala salons and clinics" or "product pages for Ugandan e-commerce" is specific and findable.
- Choose by: industry knowledge, results you can prove, client budgets, and your interest.
- Write your **one-line position:** "I help [who] get [result] with [mechanism/service]."
- Have an honest answer to "why you over a template or ChatGPT?": research, strategy, local insight, accountability, and results.

## 2. Services and productised packages

Productise to avoid hourly pricing. Examples:
- **Messaging and positioning sprint:** research summary, positioning, offer, messaging house.
- **Homepage/landing page rewrite:** audit, strategy, copy, handoff.
- **Product description pack:** template + 20 hero products + guidelines.
- **WhatsApp sales kit:** greeting, catalogue copy, scripts, objection answers, follow-ups.
- **Email sequence:** welcome (5), abandoned cart (3), or launch (7).
- **Ad creative pack:** angles, hooks, 10 ad variants, test plan.
- **Content engine:** 30-day calendar + 12 scripts + templates.
- **SEO page package:** keyword research, brief, 3 to 5 optimised pages.
- **Brand voice and naming package.**
- **Retainer:** monthly content, emails, or ad copy plus testing and reporting.
Name packages by outcome and give scope, timeline, deliverables, and revisions.

## 3. Pricing models and how to set rates

- **Project price** (preferred): based on value, scope, and complexity.
- **Retainer:** recurring scope; predictable income; include reporting and a capacity cap.
- **Hourly/day rate:** use for undefined scope; estimate hours honestly (research and revisions included).
- **Performance/commission:** share of revenue; only with clear tracking and fair terms, usually plus a base fee.
- **Value anchoring:** price relative to the client's expected gain (a page that brings 5 extra sales a week is worth more than the hours it takes).

Setting rates: target monthly income / billable hours available (usually 50 to 60 percent of working hours) = minimum hourly equivalent; adjust for market, experience, niche, and value. Raise prices as demand and proof grow; do not compete on being cheapest.

## 4. Finding clients (especially in Uganda/East Africa)

- **Local businesses you can reach in person:** salons, shops, clinics, schools, restaurants, hardware stores, mobile money agents, tour operators, real-estate agents, farms, SACCOs.
- **Offer a free, specific mini-audit:** "Here are 3 changes to your WhatsApp catalogue that could increase orders." Deliver value, then propose a paid package.
- **Referral networks:** classmates, lecturers, family businesses, church and community groups, hubs and incubators, chambers and associations.
- **Online:** LinkedIn, X, Facebook groups of business owners, Upwork/Fiverr/regional job boards, communities for startups and developers.
- **Content proof:** short posts showing before/after rewrites (with permission), lessons from audits.
- **Partnerships:** designers, developers, photographers, ad managers, and printers who meet clients needing copy.
- **Outreach:** use the outreach structure in `08-sales-playbook.md`; personalised, short, one clear ask.

## 5. Discovery call and proposal

**Discovery call agenda (30 min):** goals and numbers -> current situation and what has been tried -> customer and offer -> proof available -> constraints and timeline -> budget range -> decision process.

**Proposal structure:** their problem and goal (in their words) -> recommended approach -> deliverables -> timeline -> investment (options) -> proof -> terms -> next step. Send within 24 to 48 hours; follow up with the cadence in `08`.

## 6. Contracts, deposits, and scope control

- Use a **written agreement:** scope, deliverables, timeline, revisions (for example 2 rounds), payment schedule (commonly 50 percent deposit, balance on delivery), ownership and usage rights (transfer upon full payment), confidentiality, client responsibilities (feedback and assets on time), termination, and a statement that results depend on factors outside the writer's control.
- **Scope control:** define what is not included; price changes or additions separately.
- **Late feedback:** timelines move with client delays.
- Have a local lawyer review a template contract.

## 7. Delivery process and quality gates

Follow the skill's phases: brief -> research -> strategy -> draft -> QA -> handoff. Quality gates: research summary approved, strategy approved before drafting, scorecard of 40+/50, proof flags resolved, compliance check, and a clean handoff pack (final copy, usage guide, test plan).

## 8. Portfolio and proof when you are starting

- Create **spec work** for real local businesses (rewrite a real page), clearly labelled and with permission if published.
- Do **pro bono or discounted pilot projects** in exchange for results data and testimonials.
- Build **case studies** with before/after, process, and numbers.
- Showcase **range:** a landing page, an email sequence, a script, an ad set, a product page.
- Publish **teardowns** and lessons.
- Collect **testimonials** with specifics.

## 9. Pricing examples and ranges (illustrative)

Market rates vary widely and change. These are **illustrative structures, not quotes**; research current local rates and the client's budget before pricing.
- Small local business: single-page or WhatsApp sales kit priced as a fixed package.
- Mid-size business: messaging sprint + page rewrite + email sequence as a bundle.
- Retainers: monthly content and email plans with a defined number of assets.
- International clients typically pay higher rates; price in a stable currency when appropriate.

## 10. 90-day launch plan

- **Days 1-14:** choose niche, define 2 to 3 packages, build a one-page site/profile, create 3 spec samples.
- **Days 15-30:** outreach to 30 local businesses with mini-audits; hold 5 discovery calls.
- **Days 31-60:** close 2 to 3 projects, gather results and testimonials, publish 2 case studies.
- **Days 61-90:** raise prices, add a retainer offer, ask for referrals, systematise templates, and consider a simple partnership with a designer or developer.

## 11. Common mistakes

- Competing on price.
- Skipping research to save time.
- Vague scope and unlimited revisions.
- No deposit.
- Promising results you cannot control.
- Not collecting proof and testimonials.
- Doing everything for everyone; no niche.
- Ignoring follow-up.
