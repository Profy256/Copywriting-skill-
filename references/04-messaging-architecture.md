# 04 - Messaging Architecture

Messaging architecture turns positioning into reusable building blocks, so every ad, page, script and caption says the same thing in different ways. Build it once, then draw from it.

## 1. The message house

```
              BIG IDEA / ONE-LINE PROMISE
   ---------------------------------------------------
   PILLAR 1          PILLAR 2          PILLAR 3
   benefit           benefit           benefit
   proof, proof      proof, proof      proof, proof
   ---------------------------------------------------
   FOUNDATION: brand truths, values, origin, guarantees, credentials
```

- **Roof:** the single sentence a customer would use to describe you to a friend.
- **Pillars:** at most three. More than three means none are remembered. Each pillar is a customer benefit (not a feature) plus two pieces of proof.
- **Foundation:** facts that support everything (years, registration, location, founder story, policies).

## 2. The big idea

A big idea is a simple, memorable concept that carries the promise and can stretch across channels. It is not a slogan; slogans express it.

Checks:
- Is it about the customer's world rather than ours?
- Is it one idea?
- Can it be shown as well as said?
- Can it generate 20 different ads without repeating itself?
- Is it true and ownable?

Techniques: name the enemy, create a ritual, give the product a character, flip an assumption ("Study less, revise smarter"), use a vivid metaphor, dramatize the before/after, use a local cultural reference sparingly and respectfully.

## 3. Value propositions (feature -> benefit -> outcome)

For each important feature:

| Feature (what it is) | Advantage (what it does) | Benefit (what it means for them) | Outcome / emotion (why they care) | Proof |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Works offline | No data needed after download | Study on the bus, in a power cut | Never miss revision time | Demo clip |

Keep climbing until you reach an emotional or financial outcome. Headlines usually live at the benefit or outcome level; features go in the detail.

A good value proposition answers, in 5 seconds: **What is it? Who is it for? Why is it better? What do I do next?**

## 4. Proof hierarchy

Strongest first. Use the strongest available and combine kinds.

1. Demonstration (they see it work: live demo, before/after, taste, test)
2. Specific results with numbers and names ("Grace cut her stock-taking from 3 hours to 20 minutes")
3. Independent third-party validation (rated reviews, regulator numbers, press, awards)
4. Specific testimonials (problem, action, result, emotion)
5. Process transparency and credentials
6. Volume and longevity ("12,000 orders delivered since 2021", if true)
7. Guarantees (the business puts money behind the promise)
8. Generic praise (weakest; avoid)

Testimonial rules: collect specifics (what was the situation, what changed, what number), get written permission, use the person's own words lightly edited for clarity, include name and context (for example first name, role and area), and **never invent or embellish**.

## 5. Voice and tone

Voice is the business's personality; tone is how it flexes by situation.

Define voice with four sliders (pick a position on each):
- Formal ------ Casual
- Serious ------ Playful
- Expert/authoritative ------ Peer/friendly
- Restrained ------ Bold

Then write: three adjectives ("direct, warm, practical"), what the voice sounds like, and what it never does. Show **do / don't** pairs.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Pay by MoMo. Download in a minute." | "We leverage seamless digital payment solutions." |
| "Your order is on its way. Rider: Moses, 07xx..." | "Dear valued customer, kindly be informed..." |

Tone shifts: sales page (confident), complaint reply (calm, accountable), promo (energetic), price rise (transparent), outage or delay (honest, specific, with next step), condolence or sensitive topics (quiet, no sales).

Reading level: write for a 7th to 9th grade reading level for the public; shorter sentences for mobile. Technical audiences can handle jargon only where they already use it.

## 6. Words to own and words to ban

**Own:** 3 to 5 words or phrases the brand repeats until they are associated with it ("same-day", "no hidden fees", "exam-ready").

**Ban (generic filler):** "quality", "best", "leading", "innovative", "solutions", "cutting-edge", "world-class", "passionate", "seamless", "game-changing", "unlock", "elevate", "empower", "revolutionary", "synergy", "one-stop shop", "customer-centric", "state-of-the-art". If you need one, replace it with a measurable fact.

## 7. Messaging by persona

For each target persona (usually 1 primary, up to 2 secondary), write a short row:

| Persona | Situation / trigger | Top pain | Top desire | Biggest fear | Best proof | Best channel | Language / tone |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

When buyer and user differ (parent/student, owner/staff, couple), write separate messages for the decision-maker and the user, because they care about different things (parent: safety and results; student: ease and social acceptance).

## 8. Objection-to-answer map

List every objection, rank by frequency and severity, and map each to a **where** (what asset answers it) and a **how** (proof, guarantee, demonstration, reframe, FAQ).

| Objection | Real fear underneath | Answer type | Copy line | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Is this a scam?" | Losing money to a stranger | Proof + risk reversal | "Pay after you see the file preview. Our shop: [address]" | Page, WhatsApp opener |
| "It's too expensive" | Value uncertainty | Anchor + breakdown | "Less than 2,000 a day" | Price section |
| "I'll think about it" | Uncertainty / low urgency | Real deadline or next-step ease | "Reserve it free until Friday" | CTA, follow-up |
| "My cousin does it cheaper" | Price comparison | Reframe on risk/results | "The cheapest option costs more when it must be redone" | Comparison section |

Never ignore the most severe objection; address it near the CTA.

## 9. Message testing before copy

Before drafting at scale, check messaging with three quick questions (ask the user to put them to 5 real customers if possible):
1. "In your words, what is this business for?"
2. "What would make you hesitate?"
3. "Which of these 3 lines would make you ask for the price?"
