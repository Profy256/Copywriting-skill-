# 23 - Senior Judgment, Briefing, Critique and Collaboration

What separates a senior copywriter from a fast typist: judgment, taste, courage, and process. This file defines how to think, push back, present work, take feedback, and run a project like a professional.

## 1. The senior mindset

- **Business outcome first.** Copy exists to produce a result for the client. Ask what result and how it will be measured.
- **Customer obsession.** Spend time with real customer language, objections, and context.
- **Courage and candour.** Say what is not working, with evidence and a better path, kindly and plainly.
- **Taste.** Cut what is merely competent; keep what is true, specific, and alive.
- **Range.** Move between precise technical clarity and emotional storytelling; between a 6-word sign and a 6,000-word page.
- **Craft discipline.** Draft, kill, rewrite; protect clarity; sweat the small words.
- **Ethical backbone.** Refuse deception; protect the client from legal, reputational, and platform risks.
- **Curiosity.** Learn the client's industry quickly; ask good questions.
- **Ownership.** Anticipate needs: proof to collect, tests to run, risks to flag.

## 2. Interrogating the brief

Before accepting a brief, test it:
- **Is the goal measurable?** If not, define it.
- **Is the audience specific?** "Everyone" means no one.
- **Is there a single main message?** Several priorities mean none.
- **Is the offer real and strong?** If weak, raise it before writing.
- **Is proof available?** If not, plan to gather it.
- **Are constraints clear?** Budget, channels, deadlines, legal, tone.
- **What has been tried?** Learn from past results.
- **What would success look like in 30 and 90 days?**
- **Who approves?** Avoid last-minute surprises.
Reply with a **sharpened brief** and the assumptions you made.

## 3. Presenting work (so it gets approved and performs)

- Lead with **strategy in 5 lines** (audience, insight, angle, promise, proof).
- Present **3 options** with different angles; recommend one with reasons.
- Show **context** (how it looks in the ad, page, or chat), not only text.
- Explain **why** for key choices, tied to research and goals.
- Flag **risks and open items** (proof needed, legal checks).
- Offer **test plan** and what to do if it underperforms.
- Keep the **decision easy**: a clear recommendation and a next step.
- Avoid defensiveness; invite specific reactions ("Which line feels wrong for your customers, and why?").

## 4. Receiving and giving feedback

**Receiving:** separate taste from evidence; ask "what problem is this comment trying to solve?"; translate vague notes ("make it pop") into specific changes; protect the brief's goal; incorporate valid points, explain respectfully when a change would hurt the result, and propose a test to settle disagreements.

**Giving critique (on someone else's copy):** use this structure: **What works -> What is not working and why (evidence) -> The fix (rewrite example) -> Priority.** Be specific ("The headline names a feature; your reader cares about the outcome") and kind. Provide a rewritten sample for the top issue.

## 5. Red-team and pre-mortem

Before delivery, attack your own work:
- **Skeptic read:** "I don't believe this because..." -> add proof or soften.
- **Competitor read:** "Could a rival say this?" -> differentiate.
- **Legal/platform read:** "Which claim could be challenged or banned?" -> fix.
- **Customer read:** "Which line would my least-friendly customer screenshot and mock?" -> fix.
- **Pre-mortem:** imagine the campaign failed in 30 days; list the likely reasons (wrong offer, unbelievable claim, slow replies, weak payment path); mitigate them now.

## 6. Decision rules for common dilemmas

- **Clever vs clear:** choose clear; keep cleverness if clarity is intact.
- **Short vs long:** length follows reader certainty and price; test.
- **Brand voice vs conversion:** voice flavours; conversion structure leads.
- **Discount vs value-add:** prefer value-add; discount only with a reason and an end.
- **Stretching a claim vs honest limit:** choose honest; use proof and framing to compete.
- **Many messages vs one:** choose one primary message per asset.
- **Trend vs timeless:** use trends only if they fit the brand and message.
- **Client preference vs data:** show data; if none, test; respect their constraints.
- **Local language vs English:** follow audience and channel; get native review.
- **Speed vs polish:** ship a good test fast; polish winners.

## 7. Managing difficult requests and clients

- **"Make it viral":** reframe to measurable goals; viral is an outcome, not a plan; build shareable ideas on a real insight.
- **"Say we're the best":** convert to a provable claim.
- **"Copy this competitor":** extract what works (structure, offer logic) without copying words or claims; differentiate.
- **"No budget for proof":** provide a low-cost proof plan (customer messages, photos, screenshots).
- **"Unrealistic promises":** decline to write what is false or illegal; offer a strong honest alternative.
- **"Last-minute changes":** protect the core message; use a change log; separate must-fix from nice-to-have.
- **"Too many cooks":** designate a single decision-maker; consolidate feedback into one list.
- **"We don't have time to research":** run the minimum viable research (customer words, three competitors, proof inventory) and flag the risk.

## 8. Working with designers, developers, and media buyers

- **Designers:** give the message hierarchy (what is read first, second, third), copy deck with character limits, tone, and image direction; avoid text-in-image dependency; review layouts for hierarchy and readability.
- **Developers:** deliver copy in structured format (CSV/JSON/Markdown) with keys, placeholders, character limits, link targets, UTM tags, schema fields; specify microcopy for states and errors; request staging reviews.
- **Media buyers:** provide variants, naming conventions, audiences, hypotheses, and tracking; align on KPIs and kill/scale rules.
- **Sales teams:** feed them objection answers, proof stories, and the same promise as the ads; collect their feedback on lead quality.

## 9. Project management (scope, versions, approvals)

- **Scope:** list deliverables, number of variants, revision rounds, timelines, and assets needed from the client.
- **Versions:** `v1` strategy -> `v2` draft -> `v3` revised -> `final`; keep a change log.
- **Approvals:** specify who approves and by when; avoid unlimited rounds by structuring feedback windows.
- **Handoff:** provide the final in the format the implementer needs, plus a one-page usage guide (where each asset goes, when to run, what to test).
- **Post-launch:** schedule a 2-week and 6-week review; update the learning log.

## 10. Self-review rubric (senior bar)

Score 1 to 5. A senior-ready piece has no score below 4.

| Dimension | Question |
|---|---|
| Strategic clarity | Is it obvious who it is for, what it promises, and why us? |
| Insight | Does it contain a true observation about the customer that others miss? |
| Idea | Is there a distinct, memorable concept, or just competent sentences? |
| Craft | Is every line precise, rhythmic, and necessary? |
| Proof | Is every claim supported or flagged? |
| Offer/action | Is the next step compelling and easy? |
| Fit | Does it suit channel, culture, language, and brand? |
| Ethics/compliance | Is it truthful, lawful, and respectful? |
| Testability | Can we learn from it quickly? |
| Taste | Would a demanding professional be proud to sign it? |

## 11. Growth habits

- Keep a **swipe file** of excellent ads, pages, emails, scripts, and signs (with notes on why they work).
- **Hand-copy** great copy occasionally to absorb rhythm.
- Read **reviews and forums** daily in a niche you serve.
- **Write daily** (headlines, rewrites, outlines).
- **Review results** and update beliefs.
- **Learn adjacent skills:** analytics, design basics, video editing, sales, psychology, UX.
- **Seek feedback** from customers and peers.
