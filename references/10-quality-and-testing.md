# 10 - Quality, Compliance and Testing

Nothing ships until it passes this file. Run the edit passes, score the copy, sweep for AI-sounding filler, check claims and compliance, then plan the tests that will make it better.

## 1. Edit passes

Do them in order; each pass has one job.

1. **Strategy pass:** Right reader? One promise? Matches positioning, offer and awareness level? Does it do the single job of this asset?
2. **Clarity pass:** Could a stranger explain what is offered, to whom, at what price, and what to do next, after 5 seconds? Cut jargon and ambiguity.
3. **Specificity pass:** Replace every adjective and vague noun with a fact, number, example, or sensory detail.
4. **Proof pass:** Every claim has evidence in the copy or a `[NEEDS PROOF]` flag. Strongest proof is near the claim.
5. **Objection pass:** The top 3 objections are answered before the CTA.
6. **Customer-language pass:** Compare to the Language Bank. Swap brand-speak for their words.
7. **Flow pass:** Read aloud; each paragraph pulls into the next; cut anything that does not earn its place (aim to cut 15 to 25 percent).
8. **CTA pass:** One primary action, clear verb and outcome, reassurance nearby, easy path.
9. **Mobile and scan pass:** Headline, subheads, bullets and buttons alone tell the story; first screen works on a phone.
10. **Polish pass:** Grammar, spelling, consistent currency and spelling variant (UK vs US), consistent names, numbers, dates, contact details.
11. **Compliance pass:** section 5.
12. **Fresh-eyes pass:** Re-read as a skeptical stranger who is busy and a little suspicious.

## 2. The 10-point scorecard

Score each 0 to 5. Target 40 or above before delivering. Any 0 or 1 in items 1, 3 or 4 is an automatic rewrite.

| # | Criterion | 5 means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Audience fit | Speaks to one specific person, in their words and situation |
| 2 | Promise clarity | The main benefit is obvious in 5 seconds |
| 3 | Differentiation | Not swappable with a competitor; has a real "only" or mechanism |
| 4 | Proof and credibility | Claims backed by evidence; no unsupported superlatives |
| 5 | Offer strength | Value is clear, risk is reduced, next step is easy |
| 6 | Objection handling | Top objections answered where they arise |
| 7 | Structure and flow | Logical, skimmable, no friction, no padding |
| 8 | Voice and readability | Consistent, natural, plain language, mobile-friendly |
| 9 | Call to action | One clear action with a reason and reassurance |
| 10 | Compliance and honesty | Truthful, lawful, respectful, no dark patterns |

Report the score and the top two fixes you made in the summary.

## 3. AI-slop sweep

Delete or rewrite on sight:
- Openers like "In today's fast-paced world", "Are you tired of...", "Look no further", "Welcome to the world of...".
- Empty intensifiers and buzzwords: "unlock", "elevate", "empower", "revolutionize", "game-changer", "cutting-edge", "state-of-the-art", "seamless", "robust", "holistic", "synergy", "leverage", "unleash", "delve", "tapestry", "journey", "next level".
- Rhetorical triples with no content ("fast, reliable, and affordable") unless each has proof.
- "Not just X, but Y" constructions repeated; excessive em dashes; lists of exactly three everywhere.
- Over-formal stiffness ("kindly be informed", "we are pleased to announce") when the audience is casual.
- Generic proof ("trusted by thousands") with no number or source.
- Identical sentence rhythm; every paragraph ending in a punchline.
- Repetition of the product name and category in every sentence.

Test: delete the brand name. Could this be about any competitor? If yes, rewrite.

## 4. Claims and proof check

For each claim, classify:
- **Verifiable fact** the business can show (keep and place proof nearby).
- **Subjective opinion** ("our favourite", clearly opinion; fine in moderation).
- **Puffery** (obvious exaggeration like "the world's best coffee"; low risk but weak).
- **Material claim** (price, result, speed, safety, savings, comparison, health, income): **must be substantiated**.

Rules:
- Do not invent testimonials, numbers, reviews, awards, press mentions, or client names.
- Use placeholders: `[NEEDS PROOF: number of customers served since 2021]`, and list them in a "Proof to collect" table for the user.
- Comparisons must be fair and provable; do not name competitors negatively without evidence and legal advice.
- Results claims need typical-results context ("Results vary; our average student improved by X (n=...)").
- Time-limited offers must be genuinely time-limited and state the end date.
- Prices and conditions: state fees, exclusions, minimums.
- Testimonials need permission; do not alter meaning.

## 5. Compliance by category

Always verify the specifics with the relevant regulator or a qualified local professional. Treat the list below as prompts, not legal advice.

| Category | Extra care |
|---|---|
| Health, medicine, supplements, cosmetics | No cure/guarantee claims; claims must be supported; follow regulator rules; age restrictions; no misleading before/after |
| Finance, lending, investing, insurance, crypto | No guaranteed returns; disclose fees, interest, risks; show licensing; avoid urgency pressure; avoid "get rich" framing |
| Betting, alcohol, tobacco, adult products | Age gating; responsible-use messages; platform and local restrictions |
| Education and training | Accreditation and outcome claims must be accurate; fee transparency |
| Real estate and land | Accurate title, size, price, approvals; no false scarcity |
| Employment and recruitment | No fees-for-jobs scams; clear terms; non-discriminatory language |
| Children and vulnerable people | No manipulative targeting; privacy and safeguarding |
| Political, religious, social causes | Neutral, respectful, lawful; avoid exploitation of fear or grief |
| Charity and fundraising | Accurate impact claims; transparent use of funds; dignity of beneficiaries |
| Content, software, media | Only sell or distribute what you have rights to; correct licensing for fonts, images, music |
| Data and messaging | Consent, opt-out, data minimisation, secure storage |

If the business model itself looks legally risky (for example reselling copyrighted material, unlicensed financial services, unapproved medical claims), say so plainly once, explain the exposure (legal, payment provider, platform bans, reputation), and offer a compliant repositioning. Do not write copy that conceals the risk from customers.

## 6. Accessibility and readability

- Contrast and font size adequate; do not rely on colour alone.
- Alt text for images; captions for video; transcripts where useful.
- Plain language, short sentences, defined acronyms; avoid idioms that do not translate.
- Link text that describes the destination; buttons large enough to tap.
- Inclusive language; avoid stereotypes and ableist terms.

## 7. Testing plan

Test the highest-leverage variables first:

1. **Offer** (price, guarantee, bundle, bonus, payment terms)
2. **Audience and angle** (who and why)
3. **Headline / hook / first line**
4. **Proof elements and placement**
5. **CTA wording and placement**
6. **Format, length, layout, imagery**

Method by traffic level:
- **High volume (thousands of visits or impressions per variant):** classic A/B tests; one change at a time; pre-define the metric and a minimum run time (at least one full business cycle, such as a week); do not stop early on a lucky streak.
- **Low volume (most small businesses):** use directional signals, not statistical proof. Run sequential tests (week A vs week B, same conditions), use unique codes/numbers per version, use click-to-WhatsApp reply rate as a fast metric, and collect qualitative feedback (ask every new customer "What made you contact us?").
- **Offline:** unique phone numbers, QR codes, promo codes ("Say 'RADIO'"), A/B flyers by area, and quick customer surveys.

Test plan template:

```markdown
## Test [#]: [Name]
Hypothesis: If we [change], then [metric] will [increase], because [reason from research].
Variants: A (control) / B (change)
Audience and placement:
Primary metric and minimum detectable improvement:
Secondary metrics and guardrails (refunds, complaints, quality of leads):
Duration and sample/budget:
Decision rule:
Result and learning:
Next test:
```

## 8. Metrics by channel

| Channel | Leading metrics | Business metrics |
|---|---|---|
| Landing page | Scroll depth, CTA click rate | Conversion rate, cost per lead/sale |
| Search ads | CTR, quality score, impression share | Cost per lead, ROAS |
| Social ads | Thumb-stop rate, CTR, cost per click, cost per WhatsApp conversation | Cost per sale, ROAS |
| Email | Open rate (with caution), click rate, reply rate | Revenue per email, unsubscribes |
| WhatsApp | Response time, reply rate, conversation-to-quote rate | Close rate, average order value |
| SMS/push | Delivery, click, opt-out | Conversions per message |
| Social organic | Saves, shares, replies, profile visits | Leads and sales attributed |
| Offline | Calls and walk-ins with code, QR scans | Sales per campaign |
| Sales scripts | Contact rate, qualified rate, objection frequency | Win rate, deal size, cycle time |

Track both **quantity** and **quality** (leads that buy, repeat purchase, refunds, reviews).

## 9. Iteration protocol and learning log

After each test or campaign:
1. Record the result against the hypothesis.
2. Ask why (look at replies, objections, reviews, lost-lead reasons).
3. Update the Language Bank, objection list, positioning and messaging files.
4. Choose the next test.
5. Keep a learning log in `./copy-project/08-learning-log.md` with date, test, result, insight, and action.

Retire what loses; scale what wins; periodically re-run research (customers, competitors, price) because markets change.
