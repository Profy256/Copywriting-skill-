## 1. Brand platform

The platform answers:
- **Who are we for?** (audience)
- **What do we stand for?** (purpose, values)
- **What do we offer?** (promise)
- **Why us?** (differentiation + proof)
- **How do we sound and look?** (voice, identity)
- **What do we never do?** (boundaries)

Template: Positioning statement (see `03-positioning-and-offer.md`) + Brand promise (one sentence) + 3 pillars + personality + voice + proof + visual direction.

## 2. Purpose, vision, mission, values (made useful)

- **Purpose:** why the business exists beyond profit; should be specific and believable, not corporate fog.
- **Vision:** the future you are working toward.
- **Mission:** what you do, for whom, to achieve that.
- **Values:** 3 to 5, each phrased as a **behaviour with an example** ("We answer in 5 minutes - we once drove a charger across town at 9 p.m."). Values with no behaviour are decoration.
- Test: does each statement guide a real decision? Would a competitor write the same line?

## 3. Personality and archetypes

Brand archetypes (use as a shorthand, not a cage): Creator, Caregiver, Ruler, Jester, Regular person, Lover, Hero, Outlaw, Magician, Innocent, Sage, Explorer.
- Pick one primary and one secondary; describe how they show up in words, images, and behaviour.
- Write **personality as "X but not Y"** pairs: "Confident, not arrogant. Friendly, not childish. Direct, not blunt."
- Choose personality to fit **audience expectations and category**, then differentiate within it.

## 4. Voice chart and guidelines

| Voice trait | What it means | Sounds like | Never sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| Direct | Says the point first | "Your file opens or we refund you." | "We endeavour to ensure satisfaction." |
| Warm | Respectful, human | "Welcome back. How can we help today?" | "Dear Customer, be informed..." |
| Practical | Specific, useful | "Pay by MoMo. Download in a minute." | "Seamless digital transformation." |

Include: sentence length norms, vocabulary to own and ban, humour rules, emoji rules, local-language policy, how to handle complaints, how to talk about price, formatting standards (UGX 25,000; date formats), and example rewrites (before/after).

## 5. Brand story (origin, customer, manifesto)

- **Origin story:** the moment, the frustration, the decision, the first customer, what it taught you. Keep it human and relevant to why the product is better. 150 to 300 words with a one-sentence version.
- **Customer-as-hero story:** reframe so the customer is the hero and the brand is the guide (StoryBrand).
- **Manifesto:** a short, rhythmic statement of belief; useful for About pages, video, and internal alignment. 100 to 200 words. Avoid vague grandeur; ground in specifics.
- **Elevator pitches:** 10-word, 30-word, 60-word versions.

## 6. Naming and tagline process (pointer)

See `14-creative-engine.md` sections 9 and 10. Always check domain, social handles, trademark/registry, local-language meanings, and pronunciation on radio.

## 7. Visual identity direction (for designers)

Copywriters do not design, but they brief designers so visuals support the message.

**Direction brief:**
- Audience and context of use (phone, signage, packaging).
- Personality words (3 to 5) and anti-words.
- Mood references (describe, or link to examples) and what to borrow from each.
- Colour guidance: meaning, contrast, accessibility, differentiation from competitors.
- Typography: legibility, tone, availability for web and print, language support.
- Imagery style: real people, real places, product in use; avoid generic stock; diversity and local authenticity.
- Logo needs: simple, scalable, works small, in one colour, on signs, on social avatars, and in WhatsApp profile circles.
- Layout principles: hierarchy, white space, mobile.
- Do/don't examples.
- Deliverables list (logo set, palette, type, templates, social kit, signage, packaging).

For a small business, start with a **minimum viable identity:** logo, 2 colours, 1 to 2 fonts, a photo style, templates for posters, status posts, price lists, and a one-page voice guide.

## 8. Brand architecture (multiple products)

- **Branded house:** one master brand with descriptive product names (clear, efficient).
- **House of brands:** separate brands for different audiences (independent, more cost).
- **Endorsed:** sub-brands with a master endorsement.
- Choose based on audience overlap, trust transfer, and budget. Small businesses usually benefit from one strong master brand with named packages.

## 10. Rebrand and repositioning

- **Diagnose** why: old position irrelevant, new audience, reputational issues, growth.
- **Keep equity:** retain name recognition where valuable; explain changes to customers.
- **Plan the transition:** announcement copy, FAQ, redirects, updated profiles, signage, supplier and customer communication, internal training.
- **Phrase carefully** when names change (e.g. "Campus Library is now Acad Uganda"): include both names in the transition period for search and recognition.
- **Test** the new position with customers before committing large spend.

## 11. Brand consistency system

- A single **source-of-truth doc** with the voice guide and approved key messages.
- **Template library** for posts, flyers, quotes, price lists, emails, WhatsApp replies.
- **Quick-reply library** for staff in the brand voice.
- **Review routine:** monthly check of all public touchpoints (profiles, listings, site, packaging, signage).
- **Onboarding for staff** on how to talk about the brand.
