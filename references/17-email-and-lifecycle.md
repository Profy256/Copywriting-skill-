## 1. Principles

- **One email, one job, one CTA.**
- **Write to one person** as if replying to a friend; plain, human, specific.
- **Earn attention each time:** every message must give value, news, proof, or a reason.
- **Sequence thinking:** emails work as a conversation arc, not as isolated blasts.
- **Mobile first:** short paragraphs, big tap-friendly buttons or links, limited images.
- **Respect the inbox:** consent, relevance, frequency, easy unsubscribe.

## 2. Subject lines and preheaders

Length: about 30 to 55 characters (check truncation on mobile). Front-load the key word.

Types and examples:
- **Benefit:** "Cut your stock-take from 3 hours to 20 minutes"
- **Curiosity (with payoff):** "The reason your WhatsApp orders go quiet on Fridays"
- **Question:** "Still paying 150k for a book you'll use once?"
- **Number/list:** "5 things to check before buying a used phone"
- **Urgency (real):** "Slots close tonight at 6 p.m."
- **Personal/plain:** "Quick question about your shop"
- **News:** "New: pay school fees by MoMo"
- **Story tease:** "She nearly closed her salon in March"
- **Proof:** "How Mama Joy sold out in 2 days"
- **Command:** "Reply with your course unit"

Rules: match the content; avoid spam triggers and ALL CAPS; do not use misleading "Re:" or "Fwd:"; use the **preheader** to add a second hook, not repeat; test 2 to 3 subject lines per send.

## 3. Email anatomy

1. **Opening line:** connects to the subject and the reader's situation.
2. **Body:** one idea; short paragraphs; benefits; a micro-story or example.
3. **Proof:** quote, number, or demonstration.
4. **CTA:** single, specific, repeated once if the email is long.
5. **Sign-off:** a real person (name, role, reply address); a P.S. with a second reason or reminder.
6. **Footer:** address, unsubscribe, preferences.

Plain-text style often outperforms heavy templates for relationship emails; use designed emails for catalogues, receipts and visual products.

## 4. Core sequences (full outlines)

### A. Welcome sequence (5 emails over 10 days)
1. **Immediate:** deliver the promised lead magnet or welcome offer; set expectations; ask one reply question.
2. **Day 1-2:** your story and why you do this (short, human, relevant).
3. **Day 3-4:** quick win or best-of content that solves a small problem.
4. **Day 6-7:** proof (customer story) + soft introduction of the product.
5. **Day 9-10:** offer with a clear next step, plus an objection-handling FAQ.

### B. Nurture / education (weekly)
Rotate: tip, story, case study, myth-busting, behind the scenes, question to audience, offer. 70 percent value, 20 percent proof, 10 percent offer.

### C. Launch / promotion (see `16-long-form-launches-and-scripts.md`)
Announcement -> proof -> objection -> demo/Q&A -> deadline -> last hours.

### D. Abandoned cart / abandoned chat (3 touches)
1. **1-2 hours:** "Did something go wrong?" + help link + cart summary.
2. **24 hours:** address the likely objection (delivery, returns, authenticity) + proof.
3. **48-72 hours:** incentive or urgency only if real (small discount, free delivery, limited stock) + easy WhatsApp help.

### E. Post-purchase (4 touches)
1. **Immediate:** confirmation, what happens next, timeline, support.
2. **Delivery/usage:** how to get the best result; short video or guide.
3. **Day 7-10:** check in; ask for a review; give a 1-tap link.
4. **Day 20-30:** cross-sell or refill; referral offer.

### F. Win-back (3 touches over 3 weeks)
1. "We miss you" with a genuine new reason (new product, improvement, season).
2. Social proof or a story of what they are missing.
3. Final offer or graceful goodbye ("Want to stay on the list?").

### G. Re-permission / list cleaning
Ask engaged status; remove non-responders after attempts to protect deliverability.

### H. Event / webinar sequence
Invite -> confirmation -> reminders (1 day, 1 hour) -> replay -> offer for attendees.

### I. Review/testimonial request
Short, specific, easy: "Could you reply with 1) what was happening before, 2) what changed, 3) a number if you have one? May we quote you?"

### J. B2B nurture / outreach follow-ups
Value-first emails with relevant assets (case study, benchmark, short audit), spaced across weeks, each with one clear ask.

## 5. Segmentation and personalisation

Segment by: **behaviour** (clicked, bought, abandoned), **interest** (product category), **stage** (new, active, VIP, dormant), **source** (ad, referral, event), **value** (spend), **location**.
Personalise beyond the first name: refer to what they viewed, bought, asked, or said. Do not use sensitive data creepily.
Use **VIP early access**, **birthday offers**, and **refill reminders** for retention.

## 6. Deliverability and list health basics

- Send from a consistent, recognisable sender name with authentication set up (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) by the developer or provider.
- Grow the list with consent; avoid purchased lists.
- Keep engagement healthy: clean inactive contacts, avoid sudden big sends, keep complaint rates low.
- Avoid heavy images, spammy phrases, URL shorteners, and misleading subjects.
- Provide a visible unsubscribe link and honour it quickly.
- Warm up new domains or senders gradually.

## 7. Promotional calendar and frequency

- Plan the year around: school terms, paydays, festive periods, product cycles, and the owner's slow months.
- Cadence guide: newsletter weekly or biweekly; promotions as needed; avoid back-to-back discount emails; offer **frequency choices**.
- Keep a **content bank** (stories, testimonials, tips, FAQs) to feed the calendar.
- Review fatigue signals: falling opens, rising unsubscribes.

## 8. WhatsApp and SMS lifecycle adaptations

- Same lifecycle map, shorter and more conversational: greeting, helpful tip, proof image, offer, reminder.
- **Broadcast lists:** opt-in only; one idea per message; invite a reply ("Reply YES for the price list").
- **Click-to-chat links** with prefilled messages for each campaign to track sources.
- **Reminders** (appointments, deliveries, renewals) get high engagement and reduce no-shows.
- **SMS:** 160-character discipline; brand + value + action + opt-out; use for high-importance, time-sensitive updates.
- Respect hours (no late-night promos), frequency, and consent.

## 9. Transactional messages that sell without selling

Receipts, confirmations, delivery updates, and reminders are read more than promotions. Make them **clear first**, then add one helpful extra: care tips, how to reorder, a referral link, a review request after delivery, a related item suggestion. Never bury essential information under promotion.

## 10. Testing and metrics

- Test one variable at a time: subject line, sender name, send time, length, CTA, offer, personalisation.
- Metrics: delivery, open (treat with caution due to privacy features), click, reply, conversion, revenue per recipient, unsubscribe, complaint.
- For small lists, rely on **replies, conversions, and qualitative feedback** rather than tiny-sample statistics.
- Keep a **swipe file of your winners** and rotate the best performers.

## 11. Compliance and consent

- Obtain clear consent for marketing messages; record source and date.
- Provide opt-out in every promotional message and honour it.
- Follow applicable data-protection law and platform policies (email providers, WhatsApp Business rules, SMS regulations).
- Do not scrape contacts or add people to groups without permission.
- State sender identity and address as required.
