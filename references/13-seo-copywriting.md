# 13 - SEO Copywriting (search, local, and answer engines)

SEO gets the right people to the page; copy gets them to act. Write for the person first, then verify for search. Specs and algorithms change; verify anything time-sensitive with current official documentation when you have web access.

## 1. How search copy differs

- The reader arrives with a **specific question** already in mind. The first screen must answer or promise to answer it.
- Search pages compete in a visible list: your **title and snippet** are the ad.
- Credibility is judged on **usefulness, originality, experience and trust**, not on keyword count.
- One page = one primary topic and intent. Mixing intents confuses both Google and the reader.
- Search is a **long game**: pages improve over months; update them rather than writing and forgetting.

## 2. Keyword and intent research (method)

Without paid tools, use what is free and observable:
1. **Seed list:** the product, problem and outcome in customer words (from the Language Bank), plus local modifiers (city, area, institution, "near me", "price", "cheap", "best", "how to").
2. **Autocomplete and "People also ask"/related searches:** type each seed, record suggestions. Add letters (a-z) after seeds to surface long-tail phrases.
3. **Look at the results page:** what page types rank (guides, product pages, directories, videos, maps)? That reveals the intent Google believes is correct.
4. **Competitor pages that rank:** note their titles, headings, depth, formats, and what they miss.
5. **Forums, Facebook groups, comments, reviews:** exact questions and complaints.
6. **Free tools if available:** Google Search Console (own data: queries, clicks, positions), Google Trends (seasonality), Keyword Planner ranges, and any keyword tool the user has.
7. **Cluster** keywords by intent and topic, not by spelling.

Prioritise by: **business value** (does the searcher want to buy?), **intent match** (can our page satisfy it?), **competition** (can we realistically rank?), **volume** (nice, not first).

Output a table:

| Cluster / topic | Primary keyword | Variants and questions | Intent | Page type | Priority | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Rules: a small business should win **specific, local, high-intent** queries first ("laptop screen repair Ntinda same day") before broad ones ("laptop repair").

## 3. Search intent types and page types

| Intent | Example query | Page that fits | Copy goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Informational | "how to prepare for supplementary exams" | Guide / article / video | Answer fully, build trust, soft CTA |
| Commercial investigation | "best budget laptops Uganda 2026" | Comparison / review | Help decide; honest pros and cons; link to products |
| Transactional | "buy solar lantern Kampala" | Product / category page | Remove friction; price, stock, delivery, CTA |
| Navigational | "Brand login" | Brand, login, contact | Make it instantly findable |
| Local | "dentist near me", "salon in Kololo" | Location page + Business Profile | Address, hours, services, reviews, directions |
| Problem/solution | "phone won't charge" | Troubleshooting + service page | Diagnose, then offer the service |

Check each target page against intent: if Google shows guides and you built a product page, it likely will not rank.

## 4. On-page structure (the checklist)

- **URL:** short, readable, keyword-aware (`/laptop-screen-repair-kampala`).
- **Title tag:** about 50 to 60 characters; primary keyword early; benefit or differentiator; brand last if space.
- **Meta description:** about 140 to 155 characters; plain-language benefit, proof, and a call to action; it does not directly affect ranking but affects clicks, and Google may rewrite it.
- **H1:** one per page; states the topic in natural language and the user's benefit.
- **First 100 words:** state what the page covers and answer the main question directly.
- **H2/H3:** map to the sub-questions people ask; use natural variants and entities, not repetition.
- **Body:** complete coverage of the topic, specific examples, numbers, steps, screenshots or photos, local detail.
- **FAQ block:** real questions from search and customers, with direct 1 to 3 sentence answers.
- **Images:** original where possible; descriptive filenames and alt text; compressed.
- **Internal links:** descriptive anchor text to related pages; link to the money page from supporting content.
- **External links:** to credible sources where it helps the reader.
- **Schema (structured data):** where suitable (LocalBusiness, Product, FAQ, Article, Review); coordinate with the developer.
- **CTA:** matched to intent; informational pages get a soft next step (checklist, WhatsApp question), transactional pages get a direct one.
- **Freshness:** date, "updated on" when real; review annually.

## 5. Title tags and meta descriptions (that earn clicks)

Formulas (fill with specifics):
- `[Primary keyword] in [place] | [benefit/differentiator] | [Brand]`
- `[Number] [keyword]: [outcome]` ("7 Ways to Pass Resits: A Student Plan")
- `[Keyword]: [price/offer]` ("Laptop Screen Repair Kampala: From UGX 90,000, Same Day")
- `How to [result] ([year], [qualifier])`

Meta description pattern: **[Promise] + [proof/differentiator] + [action].** "Cracked laptop screen? We replace it in 2 hours with a 3-month warranty. Walk in at Ntinda or WhatsApp 07xx."

Rules: unique per page; no clickbait that the page cannot fulfil; include the searcher's own words; test and revise using click-through rate in Search Console.

## 6. Writing the page: depth, originality, E-E-A-T signals

Google's quality guidance emphasises experience, expertise, authoritativeness and trust. Practical signals:
- **Experience:** first-hand photos, process, real examples, "what we saw when we repaired 400 screens".
- **Expertise:** named author with credentials; accurate, specific, current information.
- **Authoritativeness:** mentions, citations, partnerships, certifications, consistent presence.
- **Trust:** address, phone, policies, transparent pricing, secure site, honest reviews, corrections.
- **Helpfulness:** the page leaves the reader with no need to search again.
- **Originality:** add something that other pages lack: data, a local angle, a template, a checklist, a comparison, a calculator.
- **Readability:** short paragraphs, subheads, lists, tables where comparison is needed.
- **Avoid** thin pages, mass-produced AI text without human review and added experience, doorway pages for every suburb with swapped place names, and copied manufacturer text.

## 8. Topic clusters and internal linking

- **Pillar page:** a comprehensive page on a broad topic ("Laptop repair in Kampala").
- **Cluster pages:** deeper pages on sub-topics ("Laptop won't charge: 8 causes", "Screen replacement cost", "Data recovery").
- Link clusters to the pillar and to each other where relevant; link from clusters to the money page.
- Plan 10 to 30 pieces in sequence by business value; publish with consistent quality rather than volume.

## 9. Local SEO for physical businesses

For local service and retail, Google Maps visibility often brings more customers than the website.
- **Google Business Profile (GBP):** accurate name, categories (primary and secondary), address and pin, hours (including holidays), phone/WhatsApp, services with prices, products, attributes, appointment link, description (first ~250 characters matter most), photos (exterior, interior, team, work), weekly posts, Q&A seeded, messaging on.
- **Reviews:** ask every satisfied customer; make it one tap (short link/QR); reply to every review in the owner's voice; mention services and areas naturally in replies; handle negatives calmly with facts and an offer to fix.
- **NAP consistency:** name, address, phone identical across site, GBP, social and directories.
- **Location pages (if several branches):** unique content per branch (staff, photos, directions, landmarks, hours, local reviews), not duplicated text.
- **Local content:** guides tied to the place ("Where to find genuine spare parts in Kisekka market"), event and community involvement.
- **Citations and directories:** relevant local directories and associations; avoid junk lists.
- **On-site:** embed the map, list service areas, add LocalBusiness schema, keep contact visible.
- **Local link sources:** partner businesses, schools, associations, local press, sponsorships.

## 10. E-commerce SEO (category and product pages)

- **Category pages** rank for broad commercial queries: add an intro (2 to 4 sentences), filter-friendly headings, buying guide section lower down, FAQs, unique text.
- **Product pages:** unique title and description, specs, variants, reviews, delivery and returns, FAQ, schema (Product, Offer, AggregateRating where real).
- Avoid duplicate descriptions across near-identical products; merge or differentiate by use case.
- Handle out-of-stock pages (keep, show alternatives) rather than deleting.
- Product images with alt text; descriptive filenames; fast loading.

## 11. Answer-engine optimisation (AI overviews, chat-style search)

More people get answers from AI summaries and chat assistants. Content that is clear, specific and trustworthy is easier to cite.
- Put a **direct answer** (1 to 3 sentences) under the question heading, then expand.
- Use **question-style headings** that mirror real queries and **definition + example** patterns.
- Provide **facts that can be verified:** numbers, dates, prices, steps, specs, with source citations.
- Use **lists, tables and step sequences** for scannability.
- State **who you are, where you operate, and what you offer** plainly (entity clarity), consistent across your site and profiles.
- Publish **original information** (surveys, price indices, case data, local guides) that assistants cannot get elsewhere.
- Keep content **fresh** and **consistent** across channels; fix contradictory information.
- Brand mentions in reputable places and reviews build the signals these systems rely on.
No trick guarantees citation; the safest strategy is genuinely helpful, accurate, well-structured content.

## 12. Technical items a copywriter must still care about

Coordinate with the developer on: page speed on mobile data, mobile usability, indexability (no accidental noindex), canonical tags for duplicates, XML sitemap, clean URL structure, HTTPS, structured data, Core Web Vitals, redirects when pages move, and crawlable text (avoid putting key copy only in images or hidden tabs). Provide the developer with exact title tags, meta descriptions, H1s, alt text and schema fields as a handoff table.

## 13. SEO in Uganda and East Africa

- Many searches are **short, informal, misspelled or mixed-language**; capture variants ("cheap data bundles", "uni fees", "past papers KIU pdf").
- **Mobile-first** and often on limited data: fast pages and concise answers win.
- **Local modifiers** (area names, landmarks, universities, markets, "near [stage]") are high-intent and less competitive.
- **Google Maps and WhatsApp** are major conversion paths: put click-to-chat buttons beside local content.
- **YouTube and TikTok search** is substantial; pair key pages with short videos.
- Consider **Luganda/Swahili** keywords where audiences search in those languages (verify with native speakers and real search suggestions).
- Content about **price and "how much"** performs: publish transparent price ranges (with disclaimers) to capture commercial intent.

## 14. Measuring and improving

- **Search Console:** queries, impressions, clicks, CTR, average position; find pages ranking 5 to 20 and improve them first (better titles, deeper content, internal links).
- **Analytics:** engagement, conversions by landing page, WhatsApp clicks, calls, form fills.
- **GBP insights:** searches, views, calls, direction requests, messages.
- **Process:** monthly review -> list pages with high impressions and low CTR (rewrite titles) -> pages with traffic and low conversion (improve CTA/offer/proof) -> pages declining (refresh content).

## 15. Mistakes that kill rankings and conversion

- Writing for keywords rather than the person; repeating the keyword awkwardly.
- Targeting the wrong intent for the page type.
- Thin or duplicate pages; mass-produced suburb pages.
- Burying the answer under a long preamble.
- No unique value versus the top 10 results.
- Ignoring titles and meta descriptions (low CTR).
- No internal linking; orphan pages.
- Slow, heavy pages on mobile.
- No clear CTA, so traffic does not convert.
- Never updating content; outdated prices and details.
