# 09 - East Africa Context (Uganda focus)

Read this whenever the audience or the user is in Uganda or East Africa. The goal is to adapt without stereotyping: use these as defaults to check against the brief and research, not as certainties. When a detail is time-sensitive (prices, regulations, shortcodes, platform rules), verify before publishing if you have web access, or flag it for the user to confirm.

## 1. Principles

- **Assume mobile.** Most of your audience will see copy on a phone, often an entry-level one, often on limited data.
- **Assume conversation.** Many buyers want to chat to confirm availability, price and trust before paying.
- **Assume caution.** Many people have seen fake sellers, fake investment schemes, and delivery or payment issues. Trust proof does more work here than clever slogans.
- **Respect and warmth** (greeting, titles, courtesy) matter.
- **Do not caricature.** Avoid forced slang, stereotypes, and "African-themed" cliches. Use the real language the customers use in the Language Bank.
- **Local relevance beats imported relevance:** local prices, landmarks, institutions and examples.

## 2. Mobile-first, low-data reality

- Keep pages light: compressed images, minimal scripts, no auto-playing heavy video, fast first screen.
- First screen: promise, price or offer, WhatsApp/call button.
- Use short paragraphs and large tap targets; avoid text inside images only (it is not searchable and hard to read on small screens).
- Offer low-data alternatives: text-only price list, WhatsApp catalogue, PDF under a few MB, lite versions.
- Consider offline use as a selling point where genuine ("works offline after download").
- Many users search in short, informal phrases and misspellings; reflect that in SEO and ad keywords.

## 3. WhatsApp and social behaviour

- WhatsApp is the default for customer service and sales. Provide a clickable "Chat on WhatsApp" link and a visible number. Prefill a message in the link ("Hi, I want to order [Product]").
- Facebook remains important (groups, Marketplace, pages); TikTok and Instagram are strong for youth; X/Twitter for news and conversation; YouTube for tutorials; LinkedIn for professionals and B2B.
- Status posts and groups are powerful and also easy to overuse. Respect consent and frequency; avoid adding people to groups without permission.
- Voice notes are common; offer them for support but keep written confirmation of prices and terms.
- Click-to-WhatsApp ads often work well for services and local retail.

## 4. Payments and money formatting

- **Mobile money** (MTN MoMo, Airtel Money) is mainstream. State it explicitly ("Pay by MTN MoMo or Airtel Money"). Show merchant/till details clearly and tell customers how to verify the account name before paying. Verify the current USSD codes, fees and merchant arrangements before publishing them.
- Also consider: cash on delivery, bank transfer, cards (Visa/Mastercard) for online and international buyers, and instalments or deposits for big purchases.
- **Currency format:** "UGX 25,000" or "25,000/=" or "Shs 25,000" are all seen locally; choose one and use it consistently. Avoid "25K" in formal contexts but it works on WhatsApp and social posts. Never mix currencies without a clear conversion note.
- **Price clarity:** include delivery fees, taxes where relevant, and what the price covers. "Delivery within Kampala: UGX 5,000" avoids a trust-killing surprise.
- **Anchors:** compare with familiar local costs (a boda ride, lunch, a week's data, a school-term book list, monthly rent) only when accurate and respectful.
- **Payment timing:** salary days (end of month), school-fee periods, harvest seasons, and festive periods change willingness to buy; schedule offers accordingly.

## 5. Trust and scam fears

Build visible proof of reality:
- Physical address with landmarks, Google Maps pin, shop front photo, opening hours.
- Business registration and tax details where applicable (company registration, tax ID); trading license; relevant professional or regulator registration.
- Real faces: owner and team photos and names.
- Real customers (with consent): photos, chat screenshots, delivery proof, receipts, reviews on Google/Facebook.
- Clear policies: returns, refunds, warranty, delivery times.
- Safe payment path: pay on delivery or after inspection where practical; deposits for custom work; "verify our merchant name before you pay"; official receipts.
- Responsiveness: fast, polite replies are themselves a trust signal.
- Warn against impersonation: "We never ask for payment to personal numbers other than [X]."

Investment, lending, and "quick money" categories attract fraud and strict regulation; apply extra caution (section 9).

## 6. Language and tone

- **English** is the common business language, used with local expressions. **Luganda, Swahili, Runyankole, Luo, Ateso, and others** matter strongly for radio, community, agriculture, and trust-building outside the urban English-speaking segment.
- **Code-switching** (English mixed with local words) is natural in speech and social media; use it only if it matches the brand and audience, and keep the message clear.
- A few common, safe words: "Webale" (thank you, Luganda), "Karibu" (welcome, Swahili), "Asante" (thank you, Swahili), "Oli otya?" (how are you, Luganda). **Always have a native speaker review any local-language copy before publishing;** tone, spelling, and dialect differences are easy to get wrong.
- Greetings and courtesy: begin with a greeting; use respectful forms for older customers ("Sir/Madam", "Mzee", "Maama" where appropriate).
- Humour: use carefully; avoid jokes that rely on tribe, religion, class or gender stereotypes.
- Slang changes quickly and regionally; do not force it.
- Radio copy should be written for the listener's ear and, for local-language stations, drafted by or reviewed with a native speaker.

## 7. Culture, seasonality and timing

Recurring hooks to consider (verify dates for the current year):
- **School calendar:** term starts, fees deadlines, exams, holidays, graduations, admissions.
- **Paydays and month-end;** festive season (Christmas/New Year); Easter; Eid; Valentine's Day; Mother's/Father's Day; Independence Day (9 October); Heroes' Day; Labour Day; Martyrs' Day; elections (be neutral and follow rules).
- **Agricultural seasons:** planting and harvest times, rainy and dry seasons.
- **Weddings and funerals:** community events with strong spending and need for services.
- **Sports and entertainment** moments (AFCON, Premier League nights, concerts) for bars, restaurants and media.
- **Power and connectivity issues:** relevant for products such as solar, power banks, offline apps.
- **Religious sensitivities:** respect fasting periods, prayer times and holy days; avoid disrespectful use of religious symbols.
- **Family and community orientation:** referrals, group buying and word of mouth are powerful; messages that include family benefits resonate.

## 8. Local media and channels

- **FM radio** is still influential, including local-language stations and talk shows. Consider sponsored segments, spot ads, and call-in shows. Track with codes or numbers.
- **WhatsApp groups and statuses, Facebook groups, TikTok, Instagram,** influencers and micro-influencers who are trusted locally.
- **Print and out-of-home:** posters, boda stickers, boards at stages and campuses, church and community notice boards, market stalls, branded umbrellas and vehicles.
- **SMS** (consent required) and **USSD** for reach beyond smartphones.
- **Community and institutions:** schools, churches, mosques, SACCOs, saving groups, cooperatives, universities, markets, associations.
- **Events and activations:** roadshows, demos, sampling, open days.
- **Referral programmes** often outperform paid ads in trust-driven categories.

## 9. Compliance and sensitivities

Rules vary and change. Treat this as a checklist of things to verify with the relevant authority or a local lawyer; do not state legal conclusions as certain.

- **Data protection and privacy:** Uganda has a Data Protection and Privacy Act (2019). Get consent before messaging, collecting personal data, or using customer photos or testimonials; provide opt-out; store data securely; do not share lists without permission.
- **Telecom and messaging:** follow the telecom regulator's rules and the networks' rules for bulk SMS and sender IDs.
- **Product standards and labelling:** food, cosmetics, electronics, and building materials may need standards marks and accurate labelling; do not use marks you do not hold.
- **Health and medicine claims:** medicines, supplements, herbal products, and medical services are regulated; avoid cure or guarantee claims; follow the health and drug authority's rules; do not use testimonials that imply cure.
- **Financial services:** deposit-taking, lending, investing, insurance, remittances, and mobile money are regulated. Do not promise returns; display licence details; disclose fees, interest rates, and risks clearly.
- **Betting and gaming, alcohol, tobacco:** restricted advertising; age restrictions; responsible-use messages.
- **Education and credentials:** accreditation claims must be accurate; do not imply recognition you do not have.
- **Real estate and land:** claims about titles, approvals, and prices must be accurate; verify documents before advertising.
- **Intellectual property:** only use images, music, fonts, texts, and content you have rights to; do not copy competitor copy; do not sell copyrighted books or media without licences.
- **Comparative advertising and testimonials:** be factual and provable; do not defame competitors; obtain consent for testimonials.
- **Political, religious, and ethnic content:** avoid exploitative or divisive messaging; follow election rules.
- **Children:** do not target children with manipulative messaging; follow safeguarding principles.
- **Platform policies** (Meta, Google, TikTok) apply in addition to local law.

## 10. Cross-border notes (Kenya, Tanzania, Rwanda)

- **Kenya:** M-Pesa is dominant; English and Swahili (with Sheng among youth); strong digital advertising ecosystem; price in KES.
- **Tanzania:** Swahili-first for mass audiences; mobile money across several networks (M-Pesa, Tigo Pesa, Airtel Money, HaloPesa); price in TZS.
- **Rwanda:** Kinyarwanda, French, English; mobile money widely used; price in RWF; strong government digital initiatives.
- For multi-country brands, localise payments, currency, language, examples, and legal claims for each market, and keep the core positioning consistent.

## 11. Local copy checklist

- [ ] Phone-readable on a small screen; first screen has promise, price/offer, and WhatsApp/call action
- [ ] Price in local currency, with delivery and inclusions stated
- [ ] Payment methods named; merchant/till info clear and verified
- [ ] Proof of reality: address, landmark, photos, registration, reviews
- [ ] Guarantee or safe payment path stated
- [ ] Language fits the audience; any local-language text reviewed by a native speaker
- [ ] Seasonal and cultural timing considered; no insensitive references
- [ ] Consent and privacy considered for messaging, photos, and testimonials
- [ ] Regulated-category claims checked
- [ ] Tracking set up (unique number, code, or link) to learn what works
