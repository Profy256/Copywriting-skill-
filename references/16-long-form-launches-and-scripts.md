# 16 - Long-Form Sales Copy, Launches, Video and Script Formats

For high-consideration or high-ticket offers, launches, and any asset where the reader needs a full argument. Short copy sells known, simple offers; long-form sells complex, expensive, skeptical ones. The rule: **as long as it takes to answer every question and objection a cold reader has, and not one word more.**

## 1. When long-form wins

- High price, high risk, or high emotional stakes.
- Complex or new products that need explaining.
- Skeptical or cold audiences.
- Offers with many components or conditions.
- When the reader cannot talk to anyone (no sales team).
Short copy wins for known products, warm audiences, low price, and clear intent. Test length; do not assume.

## 2. Long-form sales page / sales letter structure

A proven skeleton (adapt order to awareness level):

1. **Pre-headline (optional):** call out the audience ("For Kampala shop owners who sell on WhatsApp").
2. **Headline:** the main promise or the strongest hook.
3. **Subhead:** how, proof, or the timeframe.
4. **Lead:** problem-solution, story, or offer lead (see section 3).
5. **Credibility bridge:** why you, why believe this.
6. **The problem in depth:** describe their world, cost of the problem, failed attempts, emotions.
7. **The turning point:** a new insight or mechanism (why previous attempts failed; what really works).
8. **The solution:** introduce the product as the vehicle for the mechanism.
9. **What is inside / how it works:** modules, steps, deliverables.
10. **Benefits and outcomes:** specific, vivid, tied to their desires.
11. **Proof:** testimonials, results, demonstrations, data, press, certifications.
12. **Who it is for / not for:** qualify; repels wrong buyers, attracts right ones.
13. **Offer stack:** components, value, bonuses, price, payment options.
14. **Guarantee:** strong, specific risk reversal.
15. **Urgency/scarcity:** real deadline or capacity.
16. **Call to action:** exact steps to buy.
17. **Objection-crushing FAQ.**
18. **Recap and final CTA.**
19. **P.S.:** restate the main benefit, the guarantee, and the deadline; add one more reason to act.

Visual rhythm: subheads every few paragraphs, bold key phrases sparingly, short paragraphs, pull quotes for testimonials, buttons repeated at natural decision points.

## 3. Lead types and openers for long-form

1. **Offer lead:** "Get [outcome] in [time]. Here's how, and why it's risk-free."
2. **Promise lead:** big benefit + proof.
3. **Problem-solution lead:** describe the problem precisely; pivot.
4. **Big-secret / insight lead:** "The reason your [X] fails has nothing to do with [common belief]."
5. **Story lead:** a person, a moment, a stake, a turn.
6. **Proclamation lead:** news or a bold, true statement.
7. **Question lead:** a question the reader answers "yes" internally.
8. **Demonstration lead:** open with the proof.

Rule: the lead must match the reader's awareness level and earn the next paragraph.

## 4. Bullets ("fascinations") and benefit stacks

Bullets are mini-headlines: each should create interest and promise a specific payoff.

Patterns:
- **Specific benefit:** "How to [result] without [pain]."
- **Curiosity with payoff:** "The 3-minute check that tells you if a used phone is stolen."
- **Mistake:** "The #1 mistake that makes [result] fail (and the 5-second fix)."
- **Number:** "7 questions to ask any [provider] before you pay."
- **Contrast:** "Why [popular approach] costs more than [better approach]."
- **Blind-spot:** "What to say when [objection]."
- **Tool/template:** "The copy-and-paste [thing] that [result]."

Rules: concrete, true, deliverable (the product must actually contain the answer), varied length, no repeated patterns in a row.

## 5. The offer section and the close

- Present the offer as a **stack**: list each component with what it does and why it matters; show total real value only if provable; then reveal the price.
- Give a **reason for the price** ("Priced to cover [real reason]").
- Offer **payment options** and **choose-your-level** tiers.
- State the **guarantee** in plain language with the process.
- **Urgency:** real deadlines and limits only.
- **CTA:** exact steps ("Click, choose your package, pay with MoMo, get access in 2 minutes").
- **Risk summary** near the button.
- **P.S.** repeats the strongest reason to act.

## 6. Video sales letter (VSL) and sales video scripts

Structure (5 to 20 minutes depending on offer):
1. **Hook (0-10s):** callout + promise + curiosity.
2. **Problem and agitation:** relatable scenes, costs.
3. **Failed solutions:** why old ways fail.
4. **Discovery / mechanism:** the insight or method.
5. **Proof:** results, demos, testimonials.
6. **The offer:** what they get, bonuses, price, guarantee.
7. **Call to action:** repeated; on-screen text.
8. **Objection handling and recap.**
9. **Final CTA with urgency.**

Script format: two columns (Visual/On-screen | Narration), timecodes, b-roll notes, text overlays, music/sfx cues, and a captions-friendly version for sound-off. For short sales videos (60 to 120 s), compress to Hook -> Problem -> Solution -> Proof -> Offer -> CTA.

## 7. Webinar / workshop / live demo scripts

- **Promise the transformation** in the invitation; give a clear agenda.
- **Structure:** welcome and credibility (3 min) -> story and big promise (5) -> teach 3 key insights (20 to 30) -> bridge to the offer (3) -> offer and guarantee (10) -> Q&A with objections (10) -> final CTA.
- **Engagement:** polls, questions in chat, calls to action to comment, mini-wins.
- **Follow-up:** replay, recap, deadline reminders, personalised messages for attendees who engaged.
- For **live demos** (physical or online): choose a relatable scenario, show the "before pain", perform the demo with visible results, show the price and ordering, give a limited-time attendee bonus.

## 8. Launch sequences (product, event, enrolment)

A launch is a structured period of rising attention and a deadline.

| Phase | Goal | Assets |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-launch (1-3 weeks) | Build anticipation, collect leads | Teaser posts, waitlist page, behind-the-scenes, polls, free lead magnet |
| Open/Launch (3-7 days) | Convert warm audience | Announcement, demo or webinar, FAQ, testimonials, offer, daily email/WhatsApp series |
| Mid-launch | Handle objections | Case studies, Q&A, comparison, guarantee reminder |
| Close | Create real urgency | Deadline reminders, last-call messages, capacity updates |
| Post-launch | Onboard, collect proof, nurture non-buyers | Welcome series, testimonials, "what's next", waitlist for next round |

**Sample 7-day email/WhatsApp sequence:** Day 1 announce (problem, promise, link) -> Day 2 story/proof -> Day 3 how it works + objection -> Day 4 Q&A + testimonial -> Day 5 bonus or demo -> Day 6 deadline in 24h -> Day 7 final-hours messages (morning, afternoon, last hour).

Rules: real scarcity, honest bonuses, deliver what you promise, communicate changes transparently, and plan fulfilment before launch.

## 9. Advertorials and native content

An advertorial reads like an article but sells. Use it only with clear disclosure where required. Structure: editorial headline -> story or problem -> investigation/insight -> introduction of the product as the solution -> proof -> offer -> CTA. Keep tone informative, honest, and clearly marked as sponsored content when it is.

## 10. Lead magnets and opt-in pages

**Good lead magnets** solve a narrow problem fast: checklists, templates, price lists, calculators, mini-guides, sample lessons, swipe files, quizzes, free consultations, trial access.
- Name by outcome: "The 7-Point Checklist for Buying a Used Laptop in Kampala".
- Opt-in page: headline (benefit), 3 bullets, a visual, one field (phone/email), button copy ("Send me the checklist"), privacy reassurance.
- Deliver immediately; follow with a short nurture sequence leading to the paid offer.
- Fit the magnet to the paid offer (the problem it solves should be the first step toward the product).

## 11. Quizzes and calculators

- Interactive tools increase engagement and capture qualified leads.
- Quiz structure: hook title -> 5 to 8 easy questions -> result segments with tailored advice -> offer matched to segment -> contact capture.
- Calculators (savings, cost, ROI, loan, size, quantity): show a clear result, explain assumptions, and use the result to anchor the offer.
- Keep results honest and non-diagnostic where sensitive.

## 12. Case studies and proof stories

Structure: **Who** (customer, context) -> **Challenge** (what hurt, in their words) -> **Decision** (why they chose you) -> **Solution** (what you did) -> **Results** (numbers, time, money, emotion) -> **Quote** -> **CTA** ("Want similar results? [action]").
Formats: written, one-page PDF, video, WhatsApp-ready image card, slide. Get permission, verify numbers, keep it specific, and keep the customer the hero.

## 13. Podcast, audio and UGC scripts

- **Host-read ad (30-60 s):** personal experience, specific benefit, offer code, CTA; natural voice, not read-out copy.
- **UGC-style script:** a creator speaks as a customer; hook (problem), story, product moment, result, CTA. Must reflect real use and disclose sponsorship where required.
- **Radio/audio:** see `06-channel-playbooks.md`.

## 14. Newsletters and long-form content

- Consistent promise ("One practical tip for shop owners every Tuesday").
- Structure: strong subject, personal opening, one core idea, example, takeaway, soft CTA, P.S.
- Mix: value (70 percent), proof/story (20 percent), offers (10 percent), adjusted to audience tolerance.
- Grow by referrals, lead magnets, social cross-promotion, and partnerships.

## 15. Proposals and sales documents

- Open with the **client's goal and problem** in their words.
- Show **options** (good/better/best), outcomes, scope, timeline, price, terms, and **next step**.
- Add proof (case study, testimonials), assumptions, risks, and what you need from them.
- Keep it concise; one page summary first, details after.
- Make acceptance easy (signature, payment link, kickoff date).

## 16. Editing long-form

- Read the whole thing as the skeptical reader; mark where attention dips and cut or reinforce.
- Every section must either **build desire, remove doubt, or prompt action**.
- Remove repetition that does not add proof or emphasis.
- Check that every promise made in the headline is delivered in the body.
- Ensure transitions pull the reader forward.
- Verify every claim and number.
