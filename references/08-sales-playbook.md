Copywriters get attention. Salespeople turn attention into money. Use this file whenever sales happen in conversation (WhatsApp, phone, walk-in, field visits, B2B meetings), and to write the scripts, outreach and follow-ups that match the marketing copy.

## 1. What a salesperson actually does

1. **Finds** the right people (prospecting).
2. **Opens** a relationship without being annoying (outreach).
3. **Qualifies:** is there a real need, budget, authority, and timing?
4. **Discovers** the problem deeply (questions, not pitching).
5. **Presents** a solution matched to what was learned.
6. **Handles** objections and uncertainty.
7. **Closes:** asks for the decision and makes saying yes easy.
8. **Delivers a smooth handover** and keeps the promise.
9. **Follows up, upsells, gets referrals and reviews.**

The marketing copy sets expectations; the sales conversation must keep them.

## 2. Principles

- **Curiosity before pitching.** The best sellers talk 30 percent and listen 70 percent.
- **Sell the outcome and the next step,** not the feature list.
- **Match their words and pace.**
- **Make it safe to say yes:** clarity, proof, guarantee, a small first step.
- **Ask for the sale.** Many deals die because nobody asks.
- **A "no" is information;** a "maybe" is the enemy. Seek clear next steps.
- **Speed to lead:** reply within minutes. Response speed is often the biggest differentiator for small businesses.
- **Follow up** more than feels comfortable; most sales happen after several touches.

## 3. Pipeline stages and definitions

| Stage | Definition | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|
| New lead | Contact or inquiry received | Contacted within 10 minutes |
| Contacted | First reply or call made | Prospect responded |
| Qualified | Need, budget, authority, timing confirmed | Matches ideal customer |
| Proposal / quote | Price and offer sent | Prospect acknowledges |
| Negotiation / decision | Objections being handled | Verbal yes or clear no |
| Won | Paid or contract signed | Payment received |
| After-sale | Delivery, onboarding, review, referral | Customer satisfied |
| Lost | Declined or silent after cadence | Reason recorded |

Record **why** every lead is lost. Those reasons feed back into the copy.

## 4. Prospecting and cold outreach

**Principles:** relevance, brevity, one ask, easy reply, no fake familiarity, comply with consent and privacy rules.

**Cold email template (60 to 100 words):**
```
Subject: [Specific trigger or benefit] for [Company]

Hi [Name],
[Specific observation about them: 1 line, not flattery]
We help [similar companies] [achieve result] by [mechanism]. [Proof: "X cut Y by Z in N weeks."]
Worth a 15-minute call [specific day/time options]? If not you, who handles [area]?
[Name, role, phone]
```

**LinkedIn message (under 300 characters):** reference something real, one benefit, a low-pressure question. Do not pitch in the connection request.

**WhatsApp first message (only to people who expect or have opted in, or when contact came via a business context):**
```
Hi [Name], this is [Your name] from [Business]. You asked about [X] / [Reason for contact]. Quick question: [qualifying question]?
```

**Phone call opener:**
```
Hi [Name], it's [Your name] from [Business]. I know I'm calling unexpectedly. Do you have 30 seconds for me to say why I called, and then you can tell me if it makes sense to continue?
[If yes] We help [who] [result]. I'm calling because [specific reason]. Does [problem] affect you at all?
```

**Walk-in / field sales opener:** greet, one sentence of value, a question about their current process, ask permission for 2 minutes, leave a sample or one-pager with a contact and a reason to talk again.

**Referral outreach:** "[Mutual contact] suggested I contact you because [reason]." Always confirm permission from the referrer.

**Sequence idea (B2B):** Day 1 email -> Day 3 LinkedIn -> Day 5 call -> Day 8 email with a useful asset -> Day 12 short follow-up -> Day 18 breakup message.

## 5. Qualification

Check the following (adapt to B2C or B2B):
- **Need:** what problem, how severe?
- **Budget or ability to pay,** and how they pay.
- **Authority:** who decides, who influences?
- **Timing:** when must it be solved?
- **Fit:** can we serve them well? Qualifying out is a service to both sides.

Simple scoring: A = urgent need and can decide now; B = real need, timing later; C = curious or unfit.

## 6. Discovery questions

Open and layered; listen for emotion and numbers.

- Situation: "Tell me how you handle [X] today."
- Problem: "What's frustrating about that?" "What happens when it goes wrong?"
- Impact: "What does that cost you per month, in money, time, or stress?"
- Past attempts: "What have you tried? Why did it not work?"
- Desired outcome: "If this were solved, what would be different?"
- Criteria: "What matters most when choosing: price, speed, trust, quality?"
- Decision: "Who else is involved? What would you need to see to decide?"
- Timeline: "When do you need this by?"
- Budget: "What range were you hoping for?" or "What is this problem costing you now?"

Use **SPIN** (Situation, Problem, Implication, Need-payoff) for complex sales and **Sandler's pain funnel** to uncover the personal impact ("How long has this been a problem? What have you tried? How much has that cost? How do you feel about that?"). Use **Challenger** ideas (teach something surprising, tailor, take control) with sophisticated buyers.

## 7. The pitch

Structure: **Recap -> Recommend -> Reason -> Proof -> Price -> Next step.**

1. **Recap** what you heard: "So you need X by Y, and the main worry is Z. Is that right?" (Gets a yes and shows you listened.)
2. **Recommend** one option (or at most two): "Based on that, I'd suggest ..."
3. **Reason:** tie each feature to their stated needs, not your menu.
4. **Proof:** a similar customer, a result, a demo, or a guarantee.
5. **Price:** state it plainly after value; give the anchor; offer the payment path.
6. **Next step:** a specific action with a date or time.

Short script:
```
From what you've told me - [need], [deadline], [worry] - the best fit is [Option].
It gives you [result] by [date], and [Customer] used it to [specific result].
The price is [UGX X], and you can pay by [method]. If it's not what we agreed, [guarantee].
Shall we start with [first step] today, or would [alternative] work better for you?
```

## 8. Objection handling

**Framework (LAER):** **L**isten fully -> **A**cknowledge ("That's a fair concern") -> **E**xplore ("Help me understand ...") -> **R**espond with relevant proof, reframe, or solution. Then **confirm** the concern is resolved and return to the next step.

Rules: never argue; do not rush to defend; find the real objection beneath the stated one; isolate ("Apart from that, is there anything else holding you back?"); solve with evidence.

**Library:**

| Objection | Likely real issue | Response pattern |
|---|---|---|
| "It's too expensive" | Value unclear or budget limit | Acknowledge; ask what they are comparing to; re-anchor on the cost of the problem; show the cost per day; offer a smaller package or payment plan. "Compared with [alternative], what matters most to you?" |
| "I need to think about it" | Uncertainty, fear, or politeness | "Of course. What specifically would you like to think over - the price, the timing, or whether it will work?" Then address it; offer a trial or real deadline. |
| "I need to ask my boss / spouse / parent" | Not the decider, or avoiding | "Makes sense. What do you think they'll ask? I can put the key points in a short message you can forward." Offer to join a call. |
| "I'm already using someone else" | Switching cost, loyalty | "Great, what do you like about them? What would you change?" Find the gap; offer a low-risk side-by-side trial. |
| "Is this a scam / can I trust you?" | Fear of loss | Acknowledge the concern; show proof (address, registration, photos, reviews, receipts); offer a safe path (pay on delivery, deposit, test, small first order). |
| "Your competitor is cheaper" | Price comparison | Compare total cost and risk; clarify differences; hold price where value is real; offer a version at their price with a clear trade-off. |
| "Not now / bad timing" | Low urgency | Ask what would need to change; tie to a real deadline or cost of delay; set a dated follow-up; offer a small start. |
| "Send me details" | Brush-off or genuine research | "Happy to. So I send the right thing, what's the main thing you want to confirm?" Send a tailored short message plus a time to talk. |
| "I don't have the money now" | Cash timing | Offer a deposit, payment plan, smaller option, or a date when funds arrive; confirm the date. |
| "I've been burned before" | Past bad experience | Ask what happened; show how your process avoids it; stronger guarantee; references. |
| "It's not a priority" | Pain not felt enough | Quantify cost of inaction; use a story of a similar customer; ask what is a priority and tie in. |
| Silence / ghosting | Busy, lost interest, embarrassed | Follow-up cadence and breakup message (section 10). |

## 9. Closing

Closers should feel like helpful next steps, never pressure.

- **Assumptive:** "I'll book you for Thursday at 10. What name should I use?"
- **Alternative choice:** "Would you prefer the Standard or the Premium option?"
- **Summary:** recap agreed benefits, then "Shall we go ahead?"
- **Trial close** during the pitch: "How does that sound so far?"
- **Small step:** "Let's start with a 7-day pilot."
- **Risk reversal:** "If it doesn't work, we refund. Ready?"
- **Real urgency:** "Prices change on the 1st" (only if true). "We have 3 slots left this week" (only if true).
- **Silent close:** after asking, stop talking and let them answer.

Payment close: make payment trivial - provide exact steps, till number or merchant code, amount, reference, and confirm immediately after payment.

## 10. Follow-up cadence

Default cadence for a warm lead who did not decide (adapt to the sales cycle):
- **Same day:** thank + summary + next step.
- **Day 1:** quick value add (answer a question, send proof).
- **Day 3:** address the likely objection.
- **Day 7:** new reason or small offer.
- **Day 14:** check-in with a relevant resource or update.
- **Day 30:** final value message + door left open.
- **Breakup message:** "I'll stop here so I don't bother you. If [problem] becomes a priority, message me anytime. Wishing you the best."

Follow-up principles: each message adds something (value, proof, answer); never send "just checking in" alone; use the channel they prefer; respect opt-outs.

## 11. WhatsApp selling flow

1. **Instant greeting** (auto or manual): name, business, what they can ask, hours.
2. **One qualifying question:** "What are you looking for, and by when?"
3. **Recommend one option** with a photo or short video and price.
4. **Proof:** quick testimonial screenshot or delivery photo (with permission).
5. **Remove risk:** guarantee, pay on delivery, deposit, inspection.
6. **Close:** "Shall I reserve it for you? Pay [amount] to [number/merchant code] and send me the confirmation."
7. **Confirm and set expectations:** delivery time, rider name and number, tracking.
8. **After-sale:** "Did it arrive well? I'd love a short review" -> referral ask -> reorder reminder.

Style: short messages, friendly local tone, no walls of text, emojis sparingly, quick replies saved, voice notes optional. Keep records in a simple sheet or CRM: name, product, stage, next action, date.

## 12. Pricing, negotiation and discount discipline

- Know your floor price and walk-away point before the conversation.
- Never discount first. Ask "what number would make this work?" and trade something for every concession (volume, speed, payment up front, referral, testimonial).
- Prefer adding value (bonus, delivery, extra service) to cutting price.
- Present options (good-better-best) so the conversation is about which, not whether.
- If you must discount, state the reason and the condition: "This price applies if you confirm today."
- Be calm with hard bargaining; silence is a tool. Keep respect and humour.
- Collect deposits for custom work.

## 13. Upsell, cross-sell, referrals, reviews

- **Upsell** (better version) and **cross-sell** (complementary) at the moment of highest trust: right after the yes or after a good first result. Offer one relevant add-on, with the reason.
- **Referral ask:** "Who else do you know who has the same problem? I'll give them [offer] and you [thanks]."
- **Review ask:** make it easy (direct link), time it just after a positive moment, and give a script to guide a specific review.
- **Reorder reminders:** by usage cycle.
- **Loyalty:** simple (stamp card, points, anniversary offers).

## 14. B2B and high-ticket additions

- Map the buying group: economic buyer, champion, user, technical evaluator, blocker.
- Build a **business case**: problem cost, solution cost, payback, risk.
- Offer a **pilot** with clear success criteria and a decision date.
- Prepare a security, compliance, and procurement pack.
- Multi-thread: do not rely on one contact.
- Write a **mutual action plan** (steps, dates, owners).
- Proposals: lead with their problem and the outcome; keep it short; options and terms; a signature path.

## 15. Metrics and coaching

Track: response time, contact rate, qualified rate, quote-to-close rate, average deal value, sales cycle length, lost reasons, follow-up completion, review and referral rates. Review lost and won conversations weekly; update scripts and copy with real customer phrases and objections.

## 16. Ethics

Do not lie, exaggerate, apply pressure tactics, fake scarcity, hide costs, or take advantage of confusion or vulnerability. Obey consent and privacy rules for contact and messaging. A customer who feels respected buys again and refers.

```markdown
# Sales Script: [Business / Offer]

Goal of the conversation:

## Opener (15 seconds)

## Discovery questions (5-8)
## Recap + recommendation
## Proof stories (2)
## Price statement and payment options
## Objection answers (top 8, with exact wording)
## Closing lines (3)
## Follow-up messages (Day 0, 1, 3, 7, 14, 30)
## Breakup message
## After-sale: review ask, referral ask, upsell
```
