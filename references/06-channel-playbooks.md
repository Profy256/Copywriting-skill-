## 1. Website homepage

Goal: in 5 seconds a stranger knows what it is, who it is for, why it is better, and what to do.

Above the fold (what the first mobile screen must hold):
1. **Headline:** outcome for the target customer.
2. **Subhead:** what it is + who it is for + how or proof.
3. **Primary CTA** button + secondary CTA.
4. **Proof strip:** rating, customers served, logos, payment icons, guarantee.
5. **Visual** that shows the product or outcome.

Then: problem or situation -> how it works (3 steps) -> benefits with proof -> products/categories -> testimonials -> pricing or offers -> FAQ (objections) -> final CTA -> footer with address, phone, hours, registration, policies.

Rules: one primary action; navigation with 5 to 7 items; visible contact and WhatsApp; no autoplay clutter; fast and light for mobile data.

## 2. Landing / sales page

One page, one offer, one action. Typical order (adjust to awareness):
1. Headline + subhead + CTA
2. Pain or situation (reader feels seen)
3. The new way / solution (mechanism)
4. What you get (deliverables)
5. How it works (3 to 5 steps)
6. Proof (results, testimonials, demos)
7. Who it is for / not for
8. Offer, bonuses, price, payment options
9. Guarantee
10. FAQ (each answers an objection)
11. Final CTA with reminder of the outcome and real deadline
12. P.S. (restate the main benefit and the CTA; many people read only the headline and the P.S.)

Length: as long as it takes to answer every objection for a cold reader; shorter for warm readers. Higher price or less awareness means more copy.

## 3. E-commerce product page and descriptions

Structure:
- **Title:** [Brand/Product] + key attribute + variant (searchable and clear).
- **Hook line:** the outcome in one sentence.
- **Benefit bullets (3 to 6):** benefit first, spec in brackets.
- **Short description:** who it is for and the main reason to buy.
- **Specs table:** sizes, materials, dimensions, compatibility, warranty.
- **Social proof:** rating, review snippets, UGC photos.
- **Delivery and returns:** cost, time, areas covered, pay-on-delivery, return window.
- **FAQ:** size, authenticity, care, warranty.
- **CTA:** "Add to cart", "Order on WhatsApp".

Description formula: `[Who it is for] + [problem it solves] + [how] + [proof/spec] + [reassurance]`.
Cover sensory detail (feel, fit, smell, sound, taste), usage scenarios, and compatibility. Avoid copy-pasting the manufacturer text (duplicate content and sameness).

Bulk: for large catalogues, create a template with variables, then hand-write the top 20 sellers.

## 4. Pricing page and About page

**Pricing:** 3 tiers max, named for the buyer's situation (Starter/Growth/Scale or For solo, For teams), best-fit highlighted, per-period price, what is included, who each is for, FAQs about billing and cancellation, guarantee, and a "Not sure? Talk to us" option.

**About page:** it is a sales page about trust. Structure: who we help and why -> origin and mission (short) -> how we are different (proof) -> team faces and roles -> numbers -> values shown in actions -> CTA. Avoid "We are a passionate team".

## 5. Google Search ads and SEO

**Responsive Search Ads (typical limits; verify):** up to 15 headlines at 30 characters, up to 4 descriptions at 90 characters, final URL, display path, sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets.
- Headlines: include the exact keyword, a benefit, a differentiator, an offer, a location, a CTA, a trust element.
- Descriptions: expand benefit, add proof and CTA.
- Match intent: transactional ("buy"), commercial ("best", "price"), informational ("how to").
- Landing page message must match the ad (message match).
- Negative keywords list and location targeting.

**SEO copy:** one primary topic per page, title tag (about 50 to 60 characters, keyword + benefit), meta description (about 150 characters, benefit + CTA), H1 with the keyword in natural language, helpful sections answering the real questions, internal links, local entities (areas, landmarks), FAQ schema-friendly Q&As, original photos and details. Write for people first; do not keyword-stuff.

## 6. Google Business Profile and local listings

For physical businesses this often drives more sales than a website.
- **Name** as in real life (no keyword stuffing).
- **Category:** most specific primary category.
- **Description (up to 750 characters):** what you do, who for, where, differentiators, services, how to contact. First 250 characters matter most.
- **Services and products** with prices where possible.
- **Photos:** exterior, interior, team, products, menu, before/after; add regularly.
- **Posts:** offers, events, new items (weekly).
- **Q&A:** seed the top 5 questions with answers.
- **Reviews:** ask every satisfied customer; reply to all (calm, specific, grateful); show the owner is present.
- **Hours, holiday hours, WhatsApp/phone, directions, landmarks** ("Opposite X, next to Y").

## 7. Meta (Facebook / Instagram) ads

Structure of an ad: **Primary text** (hook in the first 125 characters before "See more"), **Headline** (about 27 to 40 characters displayed), **Description** (optional), **CTA button**, **Creative**.

Primary text skeletons:
- **PAS:** pain callout -> consequence -> solution -> offer -> CTA.
- **BAB:** before -> after -> bridge -> CTA.
- **Testimonial:** quote -> context -> offer.
- **Offer-led:** offer + deadline + proof + CTA.
- **Story:** 3-sentence scene -> turn -> product -> CTA.

Creative direction (write it too): thumb-stopping first 1 to 2 seconds (video) or a striking image with the promise; show the product in use; real faces and places; captions burned in for sound-off; vertical 9:16 for Stories/Reels, 4:5 for feed.

For small budgets, run 3 angles x 2 hooks, same offer, same audience, for 3 to 5 days, then keep winners. Click-to-WhatsApp ads often beat website ads for local/service businesses; align the first reply with the ad.

Policies: no personal-attribute callouts ("Are you overweight?"), no unrealistic before/after, no misleading claims, special categories need compliance. Verify current policies.

## 8. TikTok / Reels / Shorts / YouTube scripts

Template (15 to 45 seconds):
- **0 to 3 s - Hook:** pattern interrupt, bold claim, question, visual surprise, or direct callout ("If you sell on WhatsApp, stop doing this").
- **3 to 10 s - Setup:** the problem or promise.
- **10 to 30 s - Value:** 1 to 3 punchy points, demo, or story beat with visuals changing every 2 to 3 seconds.
- **Last 5 s - CTA:** one action ("Comment 'PRICE' and I'll send it", "Tap the link", "Visit us today").

Script format: two columns - **Visual / On-screen text** and **Voiceover / dialogue**, with timing. Add caption, hashtags (a few relevant ones), and a pinned comment with the CTA.

Content pillars for a small business: Proof (results, customers), Process (behind the scenes), Education (tips), Offers, Personality, FAQs/objections, Trends (only if on-brand).

Long-form YouTube: strong 30-second hook, promise the payoff, chapters, pattern interrupts every 30 to 60 seconds, mid-roll CTA, end-screen CTA.

## 9. WhatsApp (status, broadcast, catalogue, auto-replies)

WhatsApp is the main sales channel for many African small businesses. Treat it as a conversation, not a billboard.

**Status posts:** image or short video + 1 to 2 lines + price + "Reply/DM to order". Post 3 to 5 times a day at consistent times; mix offers, proof, tips and behind-the-scenes.

**Broadcast lists / channels:** only to people who opted in. Keep messages short, one idea, one CTA, with a clear reason for each message. Respect frequency (1 to 3 per week). Always give an easy opt-out.

**Catalogue (WhatsApp Business):** product title, price, description (benefit, size, delivery), photo, link/code. Keep prices current.

**Auto-replies (greeting and away):**
- Greeting: "Hi [Name], welcome to [Brand]. We help [who] with [result]. What are you looking for today? 1) ... 2) ... 3) ..."
- Away: "Thanks for messaging. We're away until 8 a.m. We reply first thing. For urgent orders call 07xx."
- Quick replies saved for price, location, delivery, payment, warranty, returns.

**Conversation flow:** greet -> ask 1 to 2 qualifying questions -> recommend one option -> price and proof -> payment details -> confirm and delivery -> thank, then ask for a review and referral (see 08).

Rules: reply fast; short messages; voice notes only when the customer prefers; never spam groups; do not add people to groups without consent.

## 10. SMS and push notifications

- SMS: about 160 characters per segment. Structure: **Brand + value + action + opt-out**. "Mama Joy's: Fresh tilapia in at 2pm today, UGX 18,000/kg. Order: 07xx. Reply STOP to opt out."
- Use only with consent; send at sensible hours; keep to 1 to 3 per week unless transactional.
- Push: 40 to 60 characters headline, 90 characters body; one reason, one action; personalise by behaviour; avoid vague nudges.
- Transactional: clarity beats cleverness (order confirmations, receipts, OTPs, reminders).

## 11. Email

**Subject lines (40 to 55 characters):** curiosity with relevance, specific benefit, number, question, name or segment. Preheader supports and does not repeat. Avoid spam triggers.

**Core flows to write:**
1. **Welcome (3 to 5 emails):** thank + deliver promise -> story/why -> best content or proof -> offer -> social proof.
2. **Nurture / education:** value, then a soft pitch.
3. **Launch / promo (4 to 7 emails):** announce -> proof -> objection -> deadline -> last call.
4. **Abandoned cart (3 emails):** reminder -> objection/FAQ -> incentive or urgency (real).
5. **Post-purchase:** confirmation -> how to use -> review request -> cross-sell.
6. **Win-back:** "Still interested?" -> new reason -> final offer or goodbye.
7. **Re-engagement / list cleaning.**

Email body: one idea, one CTA, short paragraphs, personal voice, plain-text feel, P.S. Test: would a friend send it?

## 12. Social posts and captions

- Hook line first (the preview cut-off matters).
- One idea per post. Alternate education, proof, personality, offer.
- End with a single prompt (comment, save, DM, visit).
- Use native formats: carousels for step-by-step, short video for demos, polls for engagement, text posts for opinion.
- For a 30-day plan, build a content calendar with pillars, post type, hook, CTA, and asset needed.

## 13. LinkedIn and B2B outreach content

- LinkedIn posts: strong first 2 lines, concrete lesson or story, white space, one question or CTA, no engagement bait.
- Profile (as sales page): headline = who you help + result; About = problem, approach, proof, CTA.
- B2B outreach messages are covered in `08-sales-playbook.md`.
- B2B pages: lead with the business outcome (revenue, cost, risk, time), include case studies with numbers, address procurement concerns, security and compliance, implementation steps and support.

## 14. Offline: posters, flyers, banners, signage, menus, packaging

Rules:
- **Distance and time:** a roadside sign is read in 3 seconds from 20 metres; a poster in 5 seconds; a flyer in 10.
- **Hierarchy:** (1) what, (2) benefit/offer, (3) where, (4) how to act. 7 words for a billboard headline.
- **One call to action,** one phone or WhatsApp number, QR code only if scanning is realistic and the landing page works on mobile.
- **Include:** business name, what you do in plain words, location with landmark, hours, price or offer, contact.
- **Big type, high contrast,** local language where it works best, no more than 2 fonts.
- **Signage:** name + category + one benefit ("Quick Phone Repair - Screen fixed in 30 min").
- **Menu / price list:** order items by profit and appeal, give items short appetising descriptions with sensory words, highlight 1 to 2 best sellers or combos, state prices clearly in local currency, avoid clutter.
- **Packaging:** brand + benefit + usage + proof + contact; small text must be readable; include batch, expiry and regulator marks where required.
- **Flyer layout:** headline -> short benefit list -> offer with deadline -> proof -> map/landmark -> contact. Add a tear-off or code for tracking.

## 15. Radio and audio scripts

- 30 seconds is about 70 to 80 words; 60 seconds is about 140 to 160 words.
- Structure: **Hook (first 3 seconds) -> Problem -> Solution -> Proof -> Offer -> Repeated CTA (name and number twice).**
- Write for the ear: short sentences, simple words, repetition of the brand name and contact, avoid homophone confusion, spell numbers as spoken.
- Include direction cues (SFX, tone, music) and, where relevant, local-language lines recorded by a native speaker.
- Consider a call-to-action with an easy-to-remember number or phrase, and a tracking code ("Say you heard us on [Station]").

## 16. Pitch decks, proposals, grant and funding copy

- **Investor deck (10 to 15 slides):** problem, solution, why now, market, product, traction, business model, go-to-market, competition and edge, team, financials/ask. One message per slide; headlines as full-sentence takeaways.
- **Client proposal:** summary of their problem in their words -> proposed solution -> scope -> timeline -> investment and options -> proof -> terms -> next step. Lead with the outcome, not your credentials.
- **Grant / NGO / crowdfunding:** need statement with evidence -> beneficiaries and stories -> approach and why it works -> outcomes and indicators -> budget -> organisational credibility -> accountability and reporting. Avoid poverty-porn; centre dignity and agency.
- **Business plan summary:** keep to facts, numbers and clear assumptions.

## 17. Case studies, testimonials, press releases

- **Case study:** Customer, challenge, solution, implementation, results (numbers), quote, CTA. Specific beats glowing.
- **Testimonial request script:** three questions (Before: what was happening? During: what did we do? After: what changed, ideally with a number?) plus permission for name/photo.
- **Press release:** headline, dateline, lead (who, what, when, where, why), quote, background, boilerplate, contact; newsworthy angle required.

## 18. App store and in-app copy

- App name + subtitle with keyword and benefit; first lines of description show the value; screenshots with benefit captions; ratings prompts after a success moment.
- Onboarding: one benefit per screen, show progress, ask permissions with a reason, celebrate first success.
- Empty states: explain and invite the first action.
- Error messages: plain language, reassure, give the next step.
