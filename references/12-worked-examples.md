## 1. Quick mode - salon WhatsApp status

**Request:** "Write a status for my salon in Ntinda."

**Micro-brief**
```
Reader: working women 22-35 in Ntinda who want neat braids without losing a Saturday | Awareness: 3
Promise: knotless braids done in under 3 hours, booked by WhatsApp
Proof: [NEEDS PROOF: photo of finished set + client permission]
Objection: "Will it hurt / take all day?"
Action: reply "BOOK" for a slot
Channel: WhatsApp status, image + 2 lines
```

**Variants**
- **A - Speed:** "Knotless braids, done before lunch. 3 hours, not 7. Slots this Saturday: 4 left. Reply BOOK."  *(only if 4 slots is true)*
- **B - Comfort/risk:** "Tender-headed? We start with a gentle prep and a 15-minute stop-and-stretch rule. Knotless braids from UGX 80,000. Reply BOOK."
- **C - Identity:** "For women who are done sitting all day for a hairstyle. Knotless braids, 3 hours, neat edges. Reply BOOK."

**Recommendation:** B first, because the likely strongest objection is pain; A as the weekly rotation if the speed claim is provable. **Test:** same photo, 3 days each, count replies containing "BOOK".

## 2. Standard mode - rewriting a weak hero

**Before**
> Welcome to Bright Future Academy. We are a leading institution providing quality education in a conducive environment.

**Diagnosis:** brand-first, no reader, no proof, no action, interchangeable with any school.

**After (parent reader, problem-aware)**
> **Your child is not "just average". They're not being noticed.**
> At Bright Future Academy, classes are capped at 25, so every child is seen, taught and tracked. [NEEDS PROOF: 2025 PLE results / parent quotes]
> **Visit us this Saturday** (9 a.m. open day, Kisaasi) or **WhatsApp 07xx xxx xxx** for the fee structure and instalment plan.

**Why it works:** names a parent worry, gives a specific mechanism (class size), offers a low-risk next step, and puts the fee question in the open. The proof flag tells the owner what to collect.

## 3. Full-mode snapshot - digital study-materials shop (what the process produces)

**Situation:** a pay-per-item site sells downloadable textbooks, notes and past papers to university students.

**Research findings (typical)**
- Customer language: "I can't afford the textbook", "exam is in two weeks", "the PDF I found was incomplete", "is it genuine?".
- Competitors: free PDF sites (incomplete, unsafe), lecturers' handouts, photocopy shops, friends' flash disks, bookshops (expensive).
- Biggest weakness in the current offer: inconsistent titles and authors, no course-unit structure, no refund promise.
- Risk flagged to the owner: selling copyrighted textbooks without licences exposes the owner to takedowns and payment-provider problems. Recommend building the catalogue around open-licensed books, permitted past papers and notes, and original summaries and revision packs.

**Positioning**
> For university students who need course materials fast and cheaply, [Brand] is the study library organised by course unit that gives you exam-ready materials in minutes, not weeks. Unlike random free PDFs, every file is checked to open, labelled by course and year, and replaced or refunded if it fails.

**Angles to test:** (1) Exam-in-14-days speed, (2) "Find it by your course unit, not by guessing", (3) Risk reversal ("Opens or refunded").

**Offer:** pay per file by MoMo/Airtel; bundle by semester ("Year 2 Semester 1 Revision Pack"); guarantee: "File won't open? Replaced in 1 hour or refunded."

**Hero (Variant 2)**
> **Find your course materials in 60 seconds.**
> Notes, past papers and revision packs organised by university, course and year. Pay with MTN MoMo or Airtel Money. Download instantly.
> [Search my course] - Opens or refunded.

## 4. Sales - handling "too expensive" on WhatsApp

**Customer:** "Hmm 120,000 is too much."

**Weak reply:** "It's the best price. Quality is high." (defensive, no information)

**Strong reply**
> Fair point, it's a real amount. Can I ask what you're comparing it to? 
> *(listen)* 
> If it's the cheaper one at 80,000: that one doesn't include fitting or the 6-month warranty. If something breaks in month 3, you'd pay again. Ours comes to about UGX 650 a day over the warranty period.
> If 120,000 at once is the issue, you can pay 60,000 now and 60,000 in two weeks. Should I reserve one for you today?

**Why it works:** acknowledges, explores the real comparison, reframes on total cost and risk, offers a payment path, asks for the next step.
