# 20 - Persuasion Psychology and the Masters' Principles

The durable ideas from the best copywriters, marketers and behavioural scientists, translated into rules you can apply. Use ethically: persuasion helps people decide; manipulation exploits them. Never use these to mislead.

## 1. The masters and what to take from each

| Master | Core lesson | How to apply |
|---|---|---|
| **Claude Hopkins** (Scientific Advertising) | Test everything; sell with specifics and reasons-why; advertising is salesmanship | Measure, use real numbers, give a reason for each claim, track results by source |
| **John Caples** (Tested Advertising Methods) | The headline decides; test headlines; self-interest and news win | Write many headlines; promise a clear benefit; test them |
| **David Ogilvy** | Research, big ideas, long copy that informs; respect the reader ("the consumer is not a moron, she is your wife") | Facts over fluff, strong headlines, clear brand image, specific proof |
| **Eugene Schwartz** (Breakthrough Advertising) | You do not create desire; you channel existing desire; awareness and sophistication determine approach | Use awareness levels and sophistication stages; mass desire; gradation |
| **Gary Halbert** | Know the hungry crowd; the offer matters most; write like you talk; "A-pile / B-pile" attention | Choose a starving audience; build an irresistible offer; be conversational |
| **Joseph Sugarman** | Slippery slide; every element exists to get the next sentence read; emotional first, justify with logic | Short opening lines, curiosity, seeds of curiosity, logical justification after emotion |
| **Dan Kennedy** | Direct response with accountability; strong offers, deadlines, P.S.; message-to-market match | Make every ad ask for action; track; use real deadlines; segment |
| **Robert Collier** | Enter the conversation already in the reader's mind | Start with their situation and words |
| **Victor Schwab** | Use proven headline formulas; test | Use the headline types in `05` and `25` |
| **Drayton Bird, Bob Bly** | Clarity, structure, practical craft | Use checklists, frameworks, benefit statements |
| **Clayton Makepeace, Mark Ford** | Emotional drivers, big promise, believable proof | Identify the primary emotion and prove the promise |
| **Chip & Dan Heath** (Made to Stick) | SUCCESs: Simple, Unexpected, Concrete, Credible, Emotional, Stories | Check ideas against the six traits |
| **Robert Cialdini** (Influence) | Reciprocity, commitment, social proof, authority, liking, scarcity, unity | Use honestly (see `05`) |
| **Daniel Kahneman, Amos Tversky** | System 1/2, loss aversion, framing, anchoring | Make the choice easy; frame gains and losses carefully |
| **Byron Sharp / Les Binet & Peter Field** | Mental availability; brand and activation balance | Be memorable and easy to buy; mix long-term brand with short-term response |
| **Rory Sutherland** | Value is perceived; psychology over logic; reframing | Reframe the problem, signal effort and honesty |
| **April Dunford** | Positioning is context-setting | Use frames of reference |
| **Donald Miller** (StoryBrand) | Customer is hero, brand is guide | Clarify the story in the message |
| **Alex Hormozi** (offers) | Value equation, offer stacking, guarantees | Build offers so good people feel stupid saying no (honestly) |
| **Ann Handley, Seth Godin** | Useful content, permission, remarkable products | Earn attention; make something worth talking about |

## 2. Core human motivators

Mass desires that copy channels (Schwartz, Whitman's Life Force 8, and common extensions):
- **Survival, safety, health, freedom from fear and danger**
- **Comfort, ease, convenience, saving time and effort**
- **Money: earning, saving, avoiding loss, security**
- **Status, recognition, respect, belonging, being admired**
- **Love, connection, family care, protecting loved ones**
- **Competence, mastery, progress, achievement**
- **Autonomy, control, freedom**
- **Curiosity, novelty, learning, discovery**
- **Meaning, purpose, faith, contribution**
- **Pleasure, enjoyment, beauty, fun**
- **Fairness, justice, avoiding being cheated** (very relevant where scams are feared)
- **Relief of shame, guilt, embarrassment**

Exercise: for each product, name the **primary emotional driver**, the **functional job**, and the **social job** (how it makes the buyer look to others). Write headlines for each.

## 3. Cognitive biases and how to use them honestly

| Bias / effect | What it is | Honest application |
|---|---|---|
| Loss aversion | Losses feel stronger than gains | Show what the problem costs them; "Don't lose the slot" only with real limits |
| Anchoring | First number shapes judgement | Start with the real cost of the problem or the pricier option |
| Framing | Same facts, different frames | "90% on-time" vs "10% late" - choose the true, helpful frame; do not hide facts |
| Social proof | We follow others | Real reviews, real numbers, real crowds |
| Authority | We trust experts | Real credentials, visible expertise |
| Scarcity | Limited things seem valuable | Only real limits |
| Reciprocity | We repay favours | Give a real sample, tip, or audit first |
| Commitment and consistency | We act in line with past choices | Small first step, then larger |
| Endowment effect | We value what we own | Trials, samples, "try it on", demos |
| Decoy effect | An inferior option shifts choice | Tiered pricing with clear differences |
| Default effect | We stay with defaults | Pre-select the best-fit option (not a trap) |
| Choice overload | Too many options paralyse | Offer 3 options and a recommendation |
| Present bias | We overvalue now | Fast results, immediate bonuses, small starts |
| Zero-price effect | Free is magnetic | Free samples, free delivery thresholds, free trials with clear terms |
| Mental accounting | We bucket money | Frame cost per day, per use, vs a bucket they accept (airtime, lunch) |
| Pratfall effect | Small flaw increases credibility | Admit limits honestly |
| Halo effect | One strength colours impressions | Excel at the visible touchpoints (photos, replies, packaging) |
| Curse of knowledge | Experts forget beginner confusion | Write for novices |
| Peak-end rule | We remember peaks and endings | Design a great moment and a strong finish (unboxing, confirmation) |
| Unit bias | We like complete units | "One pack for one week" |
| Labelling effect | Labels shape identity | "Smart savers club", used respectfully |
| Contrast effect | Comparison changes perception | Show before/after, versus alternatives |
| Rhyme-as-reason, fluency | Easy-to-process feels true | Simple, rhythmic, plain language (but do not use fluency to disguise falsehood) |

## 4. Decision psychology for small businesses

- **Reduce perceived risk.** For first-time buyers risk is the main barrier: guarantee, trial, small first purchase, pay on delivery, proof.
- **Make the next step tiny.** "Send a photo of your phone for a free quote" beats "Contact us".
- **Reduce choice.** Recommend a default option.
- **Speed.** Fast replies and fast delivery are persuasion.
- **Reciprocity and relationships.** Remembering a customer's name, past order, or preference increases loyalty.
- **Word of mouth and community.** In trust-driven markets, a friend's recommendation outweighs ads; make recommending easy and rewarded.
- **Visible proof of life.** A shop that posts daily, replies fast, and shows real activity feels trustworthy.

## 5. Emotion and logic

People decide emotionally and justify rationally. Structure: **hook the emotion first, then supply the logic** (proof, specs, price reasoning) so the buyer can justify the decision to themselves and others. For group decisions (families, companies), supply **ammunition** the champion can reuse: a one-page summary, comparison table, FAQ, and guarantee wording.

## 6. Attention, memory and the reading experience

- **Attention is scarce:** headline, image, and first line must earn the next second.
- **Pattern interrupts** (unexpected image, question, contrast) stop scrolling; they must lead to a relevant payoff.
- **Memory:** distinctive assets (a phrase, a colour, a character), repetition, stories, vivid images, and emotional peaks.
- **Reading patterns:** F-pattern and skim reading; bold lead-ins; descriptive subheads; bullets for lists.
- **Cognitive load:** one idea per section; remove decorations that do not help.
- **Curiosity gap:** open a gap the content closes; do not overpromise.

## 7. Trust psychology

Trust = **competence + honesty + care + consistency + proof.** Ways copy builds trust:
- **Specific details** (names, places, times) signal reality.
- **Admitting limits and trade-offs.**
- **Transparent pricing and policies.**
- **Evidence that others have gone first** (reviews, case studies, repeat customers).
- **Responsiveness and politeness.**
- **Consistent information** across channels.
- **Third-party validation.**
- **Showing the people and the process.**
- **Guarantees and visible accountability.**

## 8. Pricing psychology

- **Price anchoring** and **good-better-best** tiers.
- **Charm pricing** (9s) versus round prices: round prices signal premium and simplicity; 9s signal deals. Choose per brand.
- **Unbundled vs bundled** presentation: bundle for simplicity and perceived value; unbundle for transparency.
- **Cost per use or per day** framing.
- **Payment pain:** reduce with instalments, mobile money, or pay-on-delivery; show the total clearly.
- **Free shipping thresholds** encourage larger baskets.
- **Price endings, currency symbols, and font size** shape perception: a plainly shown price feels more honest than a hidden one.
- **Premium signalling:** fewer discounts, richer service detail, quality materials, craft story.
- **Discounts:** use sparingly with real reasons; otherwise customers learn to wait.

## 9. Persuasion limits (ethics)

- Do not exploit fear, grief, desperation, or ignorance.
- Do not lie, imply false endorsements, or fabricate scarcity or proof.
- Do not hide material information (fees, terms, risks).
- Respect vulnerable groups (children, patients, debtors, the very poor).
- Make it as easy to say no as to say yes.
- If a tactic would embarrass you if the customer saw exactly how and why you used it, do not use it.
