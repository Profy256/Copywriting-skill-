# 05 - Copy Craft: Drafting Methods

How to actually write the words once the thinking is done. Covers headlines, leads, structure frameworks, body copy rules, CTAs, persuasion principles, and editing techniques.

## 1. Drafting process

1. Re-read the brief, positioning, offer and messaging files.
2. Pick the **one reader, one action, one promise** for this asset.
3. Pick the headline type from the reader's awareness level (03, section 1).
4. Write **ugly first**: get all ideas down fast (20 headlines, 5 leads) before choosing.
5. Pick the strongest, restructure with a framework, then edit hard.
6. Read aloud. Cut 20 percent. Check each claim has proof.
7. Produce 3 variants on different angles, then recommend one.

## 2. Headlines

A headline's only job is to earn the next sentence. Test: would the target reader stop scrolling? Does it contain a specific benefit, a specific person, or a curiosity gap that is satisfied by the copy?

**Types and patterns** (fill with specifics):

| Type | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Direct benefit | [Outcome] in [time] without [pain] | "A cleaner shop in 1 hour, no need to close" |
| Number / list | [N] ways / reasons / mistakes | "5 mistakes that make your shop look closed" |
| How-to | How to [result] even if [obstacle] | "How to pass supplementary exams even if you missed the lectures" |
| Question | Are you still [costly habit]? | "Still paying 150,000 for books you use once?" |
| Problem-callout | For [specific people] who [specific pain] | "For night-shift nurses who can't sleep after work" |
| Proof | [Number] [people] [did result] | "312 Kampala shops now get paid by MoMo in seconds" |
| Offer | [Offer] + [deadline/price] | "Free site visit this week, quote in 24 hours" |
| News | New / now / finally / introducing | "Now you can pay school fees by WhatsApp" |
| Curiosity | Surprising fact or contradiction | "Why our cheapest haircut has the longest queue" |
| Command | Direct imperative | "Stop losing customers at the door" |
| Testimonial | Quote with a result | "'I made my first 1M in March.' - Sarah, Ntinda" |
| Comparison / contrast | X vs Y, "instead of" | "Cheaper than a boda, faster than a taxi" |
| Fear / consequence | What happens if you do nothing | "Every unreplied WhatsApp is a customer lost" |
| Identity | Speak to who they are | "Built for ambitious final-year students" |
| Mechanism | The reason it works | "The 3-step wash that lifts stains without bleach" |

**4U check** (score each 1 to 3, aim for 9+): **Useful** (benefit clear), **Urgent** (reason now), **Unique** (not swappable), **Ultra-specific** (numbers, names, places).

**Rules:**
- Specific beats clever. Clear beats clever. Clever only when clarity is preserved.
- Use the reader's words from the Language Bank.
- Front-load the key word for mobile truncation (first 30 to 40 characters).
- Avoid headlines that are only the brand name or only the category.
- Write at least 15 before choosing.
- Pair every headline with a **subhead** that adds who it is for, how, or the proof.

## 3. Opening leads

Six reliable lead types. Choose by awareness level.

1. **Offer lead:** state the offer straight (most aware).
2. **Promise lead:** the big benefit, backed immediately (solution aware).
3. **Problem-solution lead:** describe their problem so well they feel seen, then pivot (problem aware).
4. **Story lead:** short, specific, relatable scene (unaware or low-intent social).
5. **Secret / insight lead:** reveal a non-obvious truth that reframes the problem.
6. **Proclamation / news lead:** announce something genuinely new or important.

First sentence rule: make it so short and interesting the reader must read the second. The first line of every paragraph should pull into the next ("slippery slide").

## 4. Structure frameworks

Use as skeletons, not scripts.

**AIDA** - Attention (headline) -> Interest (relevant facts or story) -> Desire (benefits, proof, outcome visualised) -> Action (CTA). Good default for ads and short pages.

**PAS** - Problem -> Agitate (consequences, emotion, cost of delay) -> Solution. Best for problem-aware readers; do not agitate cruelly or manipulate fear unfairly.

**BAB** - Before -> After -> Bridge. Great for transformations and social ads.

**FAB** - Feature -> Advantage -> Benefit. Use to convert spec sheets into value (04, section 3).

**4 Ps** - Picture -> Promise -> Prove -> Push. Strong for landing pages.

**PASTOR** - Problem, Amplify, Story/Solution, Testimony, Transformation, Offer, Response. Long-form sales pages and emails.

**StoryBrand** - Character (customer) wants something -> faces a problem (external, internal, philosophical) -> meets a guide (brand: empathy + authority) -> who gives a plan -> calls them to action -> helping avoid failure -> and ends in success. Good for websites and brand narratives.

**QUEST** - Qualify -> Understand -> Educate -> Stimulate -> Transition. For longer educational pages and B2B.

**Slippery slide** (Sugarman) - each sentence's only job is to make the reader read the next. Use for sales letters and emails.

**Inverted pyramid** - most important first. For announcements, press releases, SMS.

**One-line formula** for micro-copy: `[Who] + [Result] + [Time or ease] + [Action]`. "Kampala shops: get paid by MoMo in seconds. Set up free today."

## 5. Body copy rules

- Write to **one person** ("you"). Never "our customers".
- **Benefits before features;** features as proof.
- **Specific over vague:** numbers, names, places, times, prices, sensory detail.
- **Show, do not claim:** replace "high quality" with the observable fact.
- **Short paragraphs** (1 to 3 lines), mixed sentence lengths, concrete nouns, strong verbs.
- Use **subheads** that tell the story on their own (a skimmer reading only subheads should get the pitch).
- **Bullets** for scannable proof or deliverables; start each with a benefit or action.
- **Handle objections** where they arise, not in a distant FAQ.
- **Use the customer's vocabulary,** not industry terms.
- **One main idea per section.**
- **Avoid:** passive voice, hedges ("might", "perhaps"), stacked adjectives, brand-centric openings ("We are a leading ..."), exclamation inflation, unexplained jargon, walls of text.
- **Numbers:** use numerals, units, and local currency format (UGX 25,000). Round only when it helps.
- **Credibility pass:** after any big claim, add the evidence within one or two sentences.

## 6. Calls to action

A CTA tells the reader the exact next step and what they get. Reduce friction and risk near it.

- Formula: **verb + outcome + (ease or risk reducer).** "Get my free quote (reply in 10 minutes)". "Book a table for tonight". "Chat on WhatsApp - we reply in 5 minutes".
- First person can lift response ("Send me the price list") but test it.
- Avoid "Submit", "Click here", "Learn more" unless the action truly is that.
- Offer a **low-commitment option** next to the main CTA for less-ready readers ("See sample" beside "Buy").
- Add micro-reassurance under buttons: payment methods, refund, no signup, response time.
- Repeat the CTA at natural decision points; do not interrupt with a CTA before the value is established.
- Physical: one action, one channel, one number. "WhatsApp 07xx xxx xxx" beats five contacts.

## 7. Persuasion principles (use honestly)

| Principle | Honest use |
|---|---|
| Social proof | Real customer numbers, real names, real reviews, real queues |
| Authority | Real credentials, years, regulator marks, expert endorsement |
| Reciprocity | Give something useful first (guide, sample, audit, tips) |
| Commitment / consistency | Start with a small yes (quiz, free sample, low-price trial) |
| Liking | Be human; show faces, owner voice, local references |
| Scarcity | Real limits only |
| Unity | "People like you" identity and community |
| Loss aversion | Show what the problem is costing them; do not exaggerate |
| Anchoring | Honest reference price or cost of the alternative |
| Pratfall effect | Admit a small limit to increase credibility ("We don't do X; we do Y very well") |
| Default effect | Pre-select the best-fit option |
| Specificity / concreteness | Exact numbers feel more credible than rounded ones |

Do not use dark patterns (confirm-shaming, hidden costs, forced continuity, fake urgency).

## 8. Storytelling

Use stories to make an abstract benefit emotional. Structure: **a person + a want + an obstacle + a turning point + a result + the lesson that relates to the reader.** Keep it under 150 words in ads and emails, with one sensory detail and one number. Origin stories work when they explain why the product is different, not just why the founder is nice.

## 9. Clarity, rhythm, readability

- Prefer 8 to 18 words per sentence; vary on purpose.
- Read aloud for flow; cut any line you stumble on.
- One idea per sentence; one purpose per paragraph.
- Prefer concrete nouns and active verbs ("We deliver" not "Delivery is provided").
- Check the **scan test**: headline, subheads, bullets, buttons, and bold phrases alone should explain the offer.
- Check the **mobile test**: first screen shows promise, proof, and action without scrolling.
- Check the **bar test:** can you say the pitch in a loud place in one breath?

## 10. Microcopy

Buttons, form labels, error messages, empty states, confirmations, notifications, WhatsApp auto-replies.
- Say what will happen next and when.
- Be human and calm in errors ("Payment didn't go through. Your money is safe. Try again or call 07xx").
- Remove unnecessary form fields; explain why you ask for phone numbers.
- Confirmation messages should reassure and cross-sell lightly, not oversell.

## 11. Rewriting weak copy

Process: (1) identify the reader and goal, (2) list what is claimed and what proof exists, (3) cut everything that fails the swap test, (4) reorder into promise, proof, offer, action, (5) replace adjectives with facts, (6) show before/after with a one-line reason for each change.

Common diagnoses:
- **Brand-first** -> flip to customer-first.
- **Feature dump** -> translate to outcomes.
- **No proof** -> add or flag.
- **Vague CTA** -> specific action.
- **Too many messages** -> one promise.
- **Buried offer** -> lead with it.
- **Jargon** -> customer language.

## 12. Variant strategy

Always provide at least three **angle-different** variants, for example Outcome, Risk-reversal, and Identity. Label each variant with its angle and the reader state it targets, and add a one-line "why this could win". Recommend one with reasoning, say what evidence would change the recommendation, and propose the test.
