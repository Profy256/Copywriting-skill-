# 01 - Discovery Brief

Goal: collect the facts that every later decision depends on, without exhausting the user. Ask in one batch. Anything unanswered becomes a labelled `ASSUMPTION`.

## Contents
1. Rapid intake (8 questions)
2. Full intake
3. Extra questions for physical businesses
4. Extra questions for online businesses
5. Extra questions for B2B and high-ticket
6. Assumption protocol
7. Brief template

## 1. Rapid intake (use when the user is impatient or the job is small)

1. What exactly do you sell, and what does it cost?
2. Who is your best customer? Describe one real person you have sold to.
3. What problem were they trying to solve the day they came to you?
4. Why do they choose you instead of the alternatives (including doing nothing)?
5. What proof do you have? (customer results, reviews, photos, years in business, numbers, certifications, guarantees)
6. What stops people from buying? (price, trust, timing, "let me think", competitor)
7. Where will the copy appear, and what single action should the reader take?
8. Anything that must be included or avoided? (tone, language, legal limits, past campaigns)

## 2. Full intake

**Business**
- Name, location(s), years operating, team size, legal status (registered? licensed?).
- Products or services with prices, margins if known, best sellers, slow sellers.
- Capacity limits (how many customers per day, delivery radius, stock).
- Business goal for the next 90 days in numbers (sales, leads, bookings, sign-ups).

**Offer**
- What is included? What is not? Delivery, installation, after-sales, warranty, refunds.
- Current discounts, bundles, payment options (cash, mobile money, card, installments, credit).
- What is the one thing you would be proud to guarantee?

**Customer**
- Who buys: age, gender mix, income band, occupation, location, device, language(s).
- Who decides vs who pays vs who uses (parent/student, boss/employee, spouse/partner).
- Where do they spend time online and offline? (WhatsApp groups, Facebook, TikTok, radio, church, campus, markets)
- What do they say before they buy and after they buy? Collect actual quotes and messages.
- Last 5 customers: why did each buy? Last 5 lost leads: why did each not buy?

**Market**
- Top 3 to 5 competitors, direct and indirect, including the informal ones (roadside, friend's contact, free alternative).
- What do competitors do better? What do customers complain about regarding them?
- Price position: cheaper, same, premium? Why?

**Proof**
- Testimonials, screenshots, before/after photos, case studies, media mentions, awards, partnerships, numbers served, ratings, certifications, regulator numbers.
- Permission status for using names, faces, logos.

**Brand and constraints**
- Existing name, logo, colours, tone, taglines, past copy that worked or flopped.
- Words they love or hate. Competitor copy they admire.
- Legal or ethical limits (regulated claims, religious sensitivities, minors).
- Budget for ads, tools, production (photos, video, printing).

**Assets and access**
- Website, social handles, Google Business Profile, WhatsApp Business catalogue, ad accounts, analytics, email list size.

## 3. Extra questions for physical businesses

- Exact location, landmarks, floor, how to find it, parking, boda/taxi routes, opening hours, public holidays.
- Walk-in versus appointment versus delivery. Peak and dead hours. Seasonality (school terms, paydays, festivals, rains).
- What does a first-time visitor see, smell, hear and pay in the first 5 minutes?
- What is the competitor next door doing? What would make someone walk past them to reach you?
- Do customers phone first? Which number? Who answers? What do they say?
- Signage, shop front, shelf layout, menu, price list: what exists, what is missing?
- Local reputation: what do neighbours say about you?
- Is there a Google Maps listing? Reviews? Photos? Correct pin?

## 4. Extra questions for online businesses

- Traffic sources and volumes. Conversion rate at each step (visit, add to cart, checkout, payment, repeat).
- Where do people drop off? What do support messages ask most?
- Pricing model, trial, refund policy, onboarding steps, activation metric.
- Email or WhatsApp list size and engagement.
- Payment methods supported and failure rates.
- Product screenshots, demo video, documentation links.

## 5. Extra questions for B2B and high-ticket

- Who are the buyer, user, budget holder and blocker? Typical sales cycle length?
- Trigger events that start a purchase (new regulation, growth, failure, new manager).
- Cost of the problem per month for the customer. Cost of switching. Existing supplier.
- Procurement steps, tender rules, references required, compliance documents.
- Typical deal size, discounting rules, contract terms.

## 6. Assumption protocol

When information is missing:
1. State the assumption in one sentence, marked `ASSUMPTION`.
2. Choose the most conservative reading (do not invent proof or numbers).
3. Continue working.
4. List all assumptions at the top of the final deliverable with a "Confirm this" checkbox.
5. For critical gaps (price, who the buyer is, what is actually sold), ask before drafting rather than assume.
